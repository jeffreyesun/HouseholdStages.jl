## High-Level Concept

This is a package for efficiently implementing household stages of the following kind.

Primitive household stages are fundamentally one of the following: 
- softmax with temperature between 0 and 1 (exogenous-markov and hard-argmax are the endpoint cases)
- affine shifts backward or forward
- pointwise multiplication backward or forward
which operate on one axis of household heterogeneity at a time.

Household stages in general are the finite span of primitive stages under the composition and product operators.

## Modular Implementation

In order to implement these, we require some general utilities for data representation, "stratification" (operating on one axis at a time, but possibly in ways that depend on the coordinates along other axes), and "generalized broadcasting" (applying stratified operations on an entire array at once). Then we can use those utilities to build and define kernels and primitive stages. We also need to implement compositions and products of stages. Finally, we can use those primitive stages to define derived stages.

Maintaining modularity and separation of these concerns is crucial for maintainability, transparency, and performance.

Therefore, the contents of src/ are as follows:

1. Layout utilities which instantiate multi-axis household state spaces, with Symbol-labeled axes, in a specified order, which can be a mixture of discrete and gridded-continuous.
2. Stratification utilities. In a sense, these can be thought of as generalizing Julia's "broadcasting". These consist of:
    a. Utilities for defining scalar-valued fields that possibly vary by axis.
    b. Utilities for defining matrix-valued fields which specify their "operative axis", that possibly vary by the **other** axes (axes other than the operative axis).
    c. Utilities for allocating, filling, caching, and refreshing the above given an `env`.
    d. Utilities for broadcasting a "(matrix, vector) -> vector)" closure, and its in-place version, over a matrix-valued field. That is, applying each matrix-valued slice of the matrix-valued field over each vector-valued slice of an array in a gridded layout to be operated on.
    e. Utilities for broadcasting a "(scalar, vector) -> vector)" closure, and its in-place version, over a scalar-valued field. That is, applying each scalar-valued slice of the scalar-value field over each vector-valued slice of an array in a gridded layout to be operated on. (See mean-preserving spread example.)
3. Kernel-application utilities. These include:
    a. Utilities for efficiently applying a matrix-valued field over its operative axis as a stratified tensor contraction, in either covariant/original or contravariant/transpose mode.
    b. Utilities for applying integer-valued scalar-valued fields as scattering along a discrete axis, also in original or transpose mode.
    c. Utilities for applying float-valued scalar-valued fields as scattering along a continuous axis.
4. Utilities for defining "moments" (integrals) as closures, and computing them given `env` and \Lambda.
5. Primitive stages:
    a. The pointwise primitive stages, hard argmax, softmax, and Markov.
    b. A few other optimization problems over slightly more exotic subspaces of big Hilbert spaces, such as continuous argmax, optimal choice of mean-preserving spread with costly effort, and bias-variance trade-off. The reason these cases are handled by separate primitive stages is for speed and auto-differentiability: the solutions to these optimization problems can be stored as per-cell floats, which are differentiable, rather than via non-differentiable references to specific destination-indices.
6. The composition operator.
7. The product operator.
8. Derived stages, consisting of composed/producted or wrapped primitive stages.

### What this is

A household's within-period problem, in this framework, is a composition of **stages** — each a
matched pair of a forward map on the distribution `Λ` and a backward map on the value `V` — glued by
two combinators: `∘` (time-ordered composition) and `⊕` (direct sum over an axis, e.g.
`replicate_age(block, N; axis=:age)`). A *model's household block* is, ideally, a `∘`/`⊕` chain of
**existing** stages with **no bespoke per-model stage** — the economics lives in the parameters,
sources, and grids handed to general stages, not in new code.

This document catalogs, as comprehensively as we can, the household problems in the heterogeneous-agent
literature that can be assembled this way — **faithfully** (the decomposition reproduces the model's
own recursion, not a near-cousin) and **elegantly** (a short chain of general stages). For each model:
the economic mechanism, the exact stage chain, a note on fidelity, and a status mark.

**The organizing principle is the *control mechanism*, not the literature area.** Two models from
different fields that let the household control the same *feature of its transition* are the same
object in this framework. The household can control:

- nothing — an **exogenous/deterministic** transition (the *spine*: §0);
- the **drift / level** of a continuous next-state — "how much" to save/invest (§1, the continuous-argmax family);
- the **dispersion / variance** of the transition — risk exposure, signal precision (§2, the MPS/mean-variance/RI family);
- the **hazard / probability** of reaching a target state — search, effort, survival (§3);
- the **direction / support** — *which* discrete destination to aim at (§4, the logit/argmax-over-destinations family);
- a **discrete jump at a fixed cost** / optimal stopping (§5(i)), possibly moving **two axes at once** (§5(ii));

with **demographics** (entry/exit/aging) handled by a small set of structural stages (§5(iii)). A short
list of **genuine household-stage gaps** — household choices the stage vocabulary cannot yet express — is
consolidated at the end.

**Scope — household blocks only.** This package builds *household blocks*: the within-period chain of
stages mapping the continuation value `V` (backward) and the distribution `Λ` (forward). It supplies
**no** outer-loop machinery — no within-period-equilibrium solver, no firm/production blocks, no market
clearing, no two-sided-matching fixed point, no distribution-as-aggregate-state recursion. *Every* model
here — Aiyagari included — needs an outer loop the package does not provide; that is the deliberate
division of labor, not a per-model shortcoming. **The catalog therefore scores the household block
alone:** ✅ means the block is a clean composition of existing stages, *whatever* equilibrium, firm
problem, or planner wraps it. (Krusell–Smith is ✅ — its household block is a shipped example — even
though closing its aggregate equilibrium, like every GE here, is outer-loop work left to the caller.)

### Legend

- **✅** — the household block is exactly a `∘`/`⊕` of existing stages (often with a built `examples/` folder, marked **🏗**).
- **◐** — expressible, but intricate (an extra belief/auxiliary axis, a multi-period or cross-generation composition, a dimensionally heavy per-leg construction) or with no example folder yet.
- **✗** — a genuine **missing household primitive**: a household choice the current stage vocabulary cannot express (named where it occurs). It is *not* used for a model's outer-loop needs — clearing, firm blocks, matching equilibria, the cross-sectional distribution are out of scope for *every* model (see **Scope**) and never count against its household block.

Time order is left-to-right under `∘` (the leftmost stage applies first on the forward sweep).

### The stage vocabulary

**Primitives** (`src/stages/primitive/`): `MarkovStage` (exogenous (sub-)stochastic transition on an
axis — income shocks, survival); `LogitChoiceStage` (soft choice over a destination via a cost
`MatrixField` + softmax; `ε>0` logit, `ε<0` soft-min/robustness, `ε→0` the hard limit);
`ArgmaxStage` (discrete `(max,+)` over a reward `MatrixField`, brute or monotone search, policy in a
`ScatterKernel`); `ContinuousArgmaxStage` (the off-grid optimum, policy a float position in an
`InterpKernel`); `DeterministicContinuousStage` (a deterministic continuous coordinate move from a
source, Young-split); `DiscreteMoveStage` (deterministic nearest-index move); `UtilityStage` (affine
shift on `V` — flow payoff, feasibility `−Inf`); `EntryStage` (affine shift on `Λ` — the forward
source `Λ += g`); `PointwiseScaleStage`/`TimeDiscountingStage` (the asymmetric scale — `β` discount,
reproduction, renormalization); `IdentityStage`.

**Derived sugar / composites** (`src/stages/derived/`): `ConsumptionSavingsStage`
(= `ArgmaxStage ∘ TimeDiscountingStage`), `WealthChangeStage`/`AssetPriceChangeStage`/`IncomeStage`
(deterministic budget moves), `AdvanceAgeStage` (sub-stochastic age Markov), `BorrowingConstraintStage`
(`UtilityStage` with `−Inf` on infeasible), `BuyHomeStage`/`SellHomeStage` (gated argmax over housing),
`DefaultStage` (gated default/repay), `MigrationStage`/`DirectedSearchStage`/`SectorSwitchingStage`
(domain-named `LogitChoiceStage` over a destination axis), `SearchMatchingStage` (effort→hazard with a
tightness externality), `CapitalInvestmentStage`/`DurableAdjustmentStage` (continuous-argmax stock investment),
`PortfolioStage`/`MeanVarianceStage`/`ScaleVarianceStage` (variance/portfolio choice over the
`MPSKernel`/`MeanVarianceKernel`), `RationalInattentionStage`, `MixingStage`/`RetentionStage` (blend
kernels at a convex cost; `RetentionStage` = the `K_A=I` "pay not to transition" member),
`LogitUtilityStage`, `KernelChoiceStage`/`ScaleChoiceStage`/`StreamingChoiceStage`, `ReproductionStage`,
`ForgetfulSumStage`, and the **exit composite** `ExogenousExit`/`EndogenousExit`/`LogitEndogenousExit`
(each `ChoiceStage ∘ UtilityStage ∘ MarkovStage` over a transient `:exiting` axis; `bequest` a required field).

**Conventions.** Reward/cost closures are *positional* start-and-end maps `(state_before,
state_after; deps…, env) -> scalar`, lowered by `to_matrix_source`; an infeasible transition returns
`−Inf` inside the closure (no separate feasibility path). `β` is always a composed `TimeDiscountingStage`
(dropped from the choice primitives). A choice that moves two axes uses an *auxiliary-choice-axis*
pattern (Route A) or a conserved-quantity reformulation (Route A′).

### §0. The spine — exogenous dynamics, level shifts, and the savings core

This family is the backbone every heterogeneous-agent model shares: state that moves *without* a choice (income/employment shocks, deterministic budget accumulation, asset revaluation, aging), the additive/feasibility *level shifts* (flow utility, taxes-transfers through the budget, borrowing limits, bequest), and the **standard-incomplete-markets (SIM) savings core** built on top. Almost everything here is one canonical chain with a different income matrix, budget closure, or constraint.

**The spine template (verified against `examples/aiyagari/model.jl`).** Every SIM model below is a relabelling of

```
MarkovStage(:income) ∘ IncomeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage          # time-ordered ∘
```

which fully expands (since `ConsumptionSavingsStage = ArgmaxStage ∘ TimeDiscountingStage`) to the four-primitive spine

```
MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:wealth) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:wealth) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β)
```

The four roles: `MarkovStage` carries the *discretized* income chain `T[y,y']`; `WealthChangeStage`/`IncomeStage` is the deterministic cash-on-hand map `b ↦ (1+r)b + w·y` (Young-split onto the wealth grid, reading prices from `env`); `ArgmaxStage` is the savings choice (`(max,+)` over next-period wealth, reward `u(b−b')` with `c≤0` masked to `typemin`); `TimeDiscountingStage` is the lone `β` discount. The borrowing limit is the lower endpoint of the choice grid; a *state-dependent* limit is a `BorrowingConstraintStage`. Each economic primitive is exactly one stage, β is isolated, and there is **no bespoke stage** (verified — `examples/{aiyagari,huggett,bewley,krusell_smith,life_cycle}` roll none).

#### Incomplete-markets savings core

- **Bewley economy (Bewley 1977)** — *mechanism:* the abstract progenitor — agents smooth Markov income against a single risk-free asset at a fixed rate, self-insuring with a precautionary buffer. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage` (fixed `r`, no clearing). *Fidelity:* the canonical spine, exactly. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/bewley`.
- **İmrohoroğlu (1989)** — *mechanism:* an early computational incomplete-markets economy — an employment/unemployment Markov, a single storage asset, evaluating imperfect insurance/inflation. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:employment) ∘ IncomeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`, benefit and storage return in the budget closure via `env`. *Fidelity:* the 2-state chain is a 2×2 `MarkovStage`; the rest is spine. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/imrohoroglu`.
- **Huggett (1993)** — *mechanism:* a pure-credit (bond) economy — the asset is a zero-net-supply bond whose price clears the credit market. *Household block:* the spine; the bond price clears in an outer tâtonnement. *Fidelity:* identical block to Aiyagari, differing only in what the outer loop clears. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/huggett`.
- **Aiyagari (1994)** — *mechanism:* the SIM workhorse in GE — idiosyncratic labor risk, a borrowing limit, and `r = `net marginal product of aggregate capital. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ IncomeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`; the firm FOCs and the `K_supplied(r)=K` fixed point are outer-loop. *Fidelity:* the reference decomposition; faithful and minimal. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/aiyagari` (and `examples/aiyagari_mit_shock` — the *same* block, MIT-shock/sequence-space transition in the outer loop).
- **Deaton (1991), liquidity-constrained saving** — *mechanism:* a buffer-stock saver facing a hard borrowing constraint and AR(1) income; the constraint binds in bad states. *Household block:* the spine with the wealth-grid floor as the limit. *Fidelity:* clean; `BorrowingConstraintStage` available if state-dependent. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/deaton`.
- **Carroll buffer-stock (1997)** — *mechanism:* impatient consumers with *permanent* and *transitory* income shocks accumulate a target buffer; solved in ratios to permanent income. *Household block:* after the permanent-income normalization, the spine with a transitory `MarkovStage`. *Fidelity:* the transitory block is exactly the spine, but the **unit-root permanent component is not a stationary `MarkovStage` on a fixed grid** — the normalization (analytic) is what makes it expressible. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/carroll_buffer_stock` — expressible only after the analytic normalization (the folder builds via that normalization).
- **Krusell–Smith (1998)** — *mechanism:* Aiyagari with an aggregate TFP shock; households forecast prices from low-order moments of the wealth distribution. *Household block:* the Aiyagari spine, `(r,w)(K,z)` via `env`. *Fidelity:* the household block is unchanged; the forecasting rule and distribution-as-state are outer-loop. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/krusell_smith`.
- **Castañeda–Díaz-Giménez–Ríos-Rull (2003)** — *mechanism:* a flexible Markov earnings process (incl. a "superstar" state) calibrated to the wealth distribution, with stochastic aging, retirement, and bequests. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:earnings) ∘ MarkovStage(:age) ∘ IncomeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`, death a sub-stochastic age Markov, newborns an `EntryStage`. *Fidelity:* the calibration richness lives entirely in `T`, not new machinery; demographics via the entry/exit composites. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/castaneda`.
- **Den Haan KS comparison (2010)** — *mechanism:* alternative aggregation methods for the *same* KS economy. *Household block:* the Aiyagari/KS spine, unchanged. *Fidelity:* the contribution is in the outer-loop aggregation; the block is verbatim spine. *Status:* ✅.
- **Krueger–Mitman–Perri (2016)** — *mechanism:* wealth inequality and the MPC distribution shape the response to aggregate/fiscal shocks; Aiyagari + employment + UI. *Household block:* the spine, employment a `MarkovStage`, UI in the budget closure, aggregate state in `env`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/krueger_mitman_perri`.
- **Auclert (2019), redistribution channels** — *mechanism:* a rate cut redistributes through unhedged interest-rate exposure and the Fisher/revaluation channel. *Household block:* the spine `∘ AssetPriceChangeStage` — revaluation is *exactly* `AssetPriceChangeStage` (`wealth += (q−q_last)·holdings`, reading `env`). *Fidelity:* an elegant fit — the package's revaluation stage *is* the balance-sheet channel. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/auclert_revaluation`.
- **Kaplan–Violante (2014), wealthy hand-to-mouth** — *mechanism:* a two-asset (liquid+illiquid) economy where illiquid holders behave as constrained HtM, generating large MPCs. *Household block:* the liquid core is spine; the illiquid adjustment is the two-asset margin (§5(ii)). *Fidelity:* liquid core ✅; the defining two-asset adjustment cross-refers to §5(ii). *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/kaplan_violante` (built via the two-asset auxiliary-axis pattern).
- **Constantinides–Duffie (1996)** — *mechanism:* heterogeneous permanent income engineered so the cross-section aggregates to an asset-pricing-matching SDF. *Household block:* the spine, no constraint, a permanent (random-walk) component. *Fidelity:* the household block composes from existing stages, but the engineered non-stationary cross-section leaves *no stationary fixed-grid object to solve* — the same fixed-grid obstruction as Carroll. *Status:* ◐ (a representational obstruction, not a missing stage — confirmed by the §0 dogfooding).
- **Discount-factor heterogeneity (Krusell–Smith 1998 stochastic-β; Carroll–Slacalek–Tokuoka–White 2017)** — *mechanism:* heterogeneity (and persistence) in `β` spreads the wealth distribution and matches the MPC distribution. *Household block:* `⊕` the spine over a `:beta` axis, each slice its own `TimeDiscountingStage(β)`; *stochastic* β adds a `MarkovStage(:beta)`. *Fidelity:* β being an isolated `TimeDiscountingStage` is exactly what makes per-type/stochastic β a clean axis replication. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/discount_heterogeneity`.

#### Income processes — the exogenous `MarkovStage`

Every model above feeds `MarkovStage` a row-stochastic `T`. The package holds the *discretized chain*; the discretization routine (Tauchen, Rouwenhorst) is an offline pre-computation, correctly *not* a stage. Where the transition varies with the aggregate state, `MarkovStage` accepts a `FromEnv` or a `(; dep…, env) -> T` closure (verified in `markov_along.jl`).

- **AR(1) via Tauchen (1986)** / **AR(1) via Rouwenhorst (1995)** — discretized persistent log-earnings. *Block:* `MarkovStage(:income; transition_matrix = T)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/income_ar1` (a plain constant-matrix Markov; Rouwenhorst superior at high persistence).
- **Persistent + transitory (Storesletten–Telmer–Yaron 2004; Kaplan–Violante 2010)** — *block:* `MarkovStage(:persistent) ∘ MarkovStage(:transitory)` (or a combined grid). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/persistent_transitory` for stationary AR(1); ◐ if the persistent piece is a true random walk.
- **Countercyclical income risk (Storesletten–Telmer–Yaron 2004)** — variance of shocks rises in recessions. *Block:* `MarkovStage(:income; transition_matrix = (; env) -> T(env.z))` — an env-closure transition. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/countercyclical_risk` (the kernel re-seats `K=Tᵀ` when `env` changes).
- **Markov/regime-switching earnings (Hamilton-style)** — *block:* `MarkovStage(:regime) ∘ MarkovStage(:income; transition_matrix = (; regime) -> T_regime)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/regime_switching` (dep-varying transitions are first-class).
- **Heterogeneous income profiles (Guvenen 2007/2009) & non-Gaussian risk (Guvenen–Karahan–Ozkan–Song 2021)** — *block:* a permanent `:income_type` axis (`⊕`) `∘` an AR(1) `MarkovStage`; non-Gaussian richness absorbed into `T`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/heterogeneous_income`.
- **Partial-insurance (Heathcote–Storesletten–Violante 2009)** — insurable + uninsurable-permanent + transitory components with progressive taxes. *Status:* ◐ — the household block composes from existing stages, but the unit-root uninsurable-permanent component crossed with progressive taxes won't normalize onto a stationary `MarkovStage`, leaving no fixed-grid object to solve (the analytic solution sidesteps the grid). A representational obstruction, not a missing stage — confirmed by the §0 dogfooding.
- **Diagnostic / extrapolative expectations (Bordalo–Gennaioli–Shleifer 2018)** — *mechanism:* agents form a *distorted* belief that over-weights states made salient by the most recent innovation (a representativeness tilt of the true kernel), over-reacting to recent news. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income; transition_matrix = (; dep…, env) -> T_distorted)` — a plain `MarkovStage` fed the *behavioral* distorted `T` (the over-weighting closure) rather than the data-generating one. *Fidelity:* faithful — the distortion lives entirely in the transition closure (`markov_along.jl` already takes a dep/`env` closure); natural- and extrapolative-expectations variants are the *same* stage with a different `T`. *Status:* ✅. (Distinct from the catalog's Bayesian-learning ◐s, which carry a tracked belief-state axis; the diagnostic distorted-`T` member needs none.) 🏗 `examples/diagnostic_expectations`.

#### Level shifts, constraints, and fiscal layers

A tax/transfer that changes *resources* belongs in the **budget closure** of `IncomeStage`/`WealthChangeStage` (reads `env`); `UtilityStage` carries *additive value* pieces (flow felicity, feasibility `−Inf`, taste shocks); `BorrowingConstraintStage` is the `−Inf`-on-infeasible special case.

- **Progressive taxation, HSV tax function (Heathcote–Storesletten–Violante 2017)** — the `y ↦ λ y^{1−τ}` schedule. *Block:* the spine with `IncomeStage(; wealth_post = (…; env) -> (1+env.r)·wealth + λ·(env.w·income)^{1−τ})`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/hsv_progressive_tax` (a one-line budget closure).
- **Unemployment insurance economies (Hansen–İmrohoroğlu 1992)** — employment risk insured by UI with replacement rates. *Block:* `MarkovStage(:employment) ∘ IncomeStage(benefit in env) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/unemployment_insurance`.
- **Borrowing-constraint variants (Aiyagari 1994: ad-hoc / natural / zero limit)** — *block:* a fixed limit is the wealth-grid floor; an income-dependent natural limit is `BorrowingConstraintStage(infeasible = (…; env) -> wealth < -income_min/env.r)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/borrowing_constraint`.
- **Limited-commitment risk sharing — Alvarez–Jermann (2000), decentralization** — *mechanism:* households trade a full set of state-contingent Arrow securities but can default to autarky, so each state's holdings are capped by a *not-too-tight* (solvency) bound that keeps continuation utility at or above the outside option. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ IncomeStage ∘ BorrowingConstraintStage(state-contingent solvency limit via env) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage` — the savings choice over the Arrow claim under a *per-state* `BorrowingConstraintStage` whose `−Inf` gate is the not-too-tight bound read from `env`. *Fidelity:* faithful — the not-too-tight constraint is exactly a state-dependent `BorrowingConstraintStage`; the rest is spine. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/limited_commitment` (the decentralization). The *planner's* promised-utility / Pareto-weight implementation is the caller's outer loop — a consistency/planner fixed point, like the optimal-UI contract boundary (§3), not a household-stage gap.
- **Preference/taste shocks and warm-glow bequest** — additive shifters and a value-of-bequest term. *Block:* `UtilityStage(utility = (…; env) -> shifter)`; bequest is the required `bequest` field of the exit composite or a finite-horizon terminal value. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/taste_shocks`.
- **Finite-horizon life-cycle / OLG (Gourinchas–Parker 2002; Cocco–Gomes–Maenhout 2005)** — age-varying income, mortality, finite horizon → hump-shaped consumption. *Block:* `replicate_age(MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage, N; axis=:age)`; `AdvanceAgeStage(absorb_top=false)` is the sub-stochastic age roll, newborn inflow from the outer loop. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/life_cycle`.

#### Monetary blocks

The catalog has carried **zero** monetary models; yet most ways money enters a household problem are spine modifications — a felicity shape (MIU), a budget gate (CIA). The one exception is search-monetary bargaining (Lagos–Wright's decentralized leg), which the dogfooding found needs a within-period primitive the vocabulary lacks (a terms-of-trade / Nash-bargaining stage). (Money's *value* — the price level, the nominal rate, market clearing — is the caller's outer loop, as for every model here.)

- **Money-in-utility (Sidrauski 1967; Brock 1974)** — *mechanism:* real balances enter felicity directly, so the household holds money despite its lower return for the liquidity services it yields. *Household block:* the spine with `ConsumptionSavingsStage(utility = (cell,c;env) -> u(c) + v(cell.money_next/env.P))` — real balances `m'` in the felicity, exactly the wealth-in-utility shape (§1) on a money axis. *Fidelity:* a one-line utility change; the start-and-end reward already sees `m'`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/money_in_utility`.
- **Cash-in-advance (Clower 1967; Lucas–Stokey 1987)** — *mechanism:* goods must be bought with money carried into the period, so consumption is capped by real balances. *Household block:* the spine with a `BorrowingConstraintStage(infeasible = (…;env) -> c > money/env.P)` — the CIA constraint is the `−Inf` budget gate. *Fidelity:* the cash constraint is exactly the `−Inf`-on-infeasible special case. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/cash_in_advance`.
- **Lagos–Wright (2005)** — *mechanism:* each period splits into a decentralized market (anonymous bilateral trade requiring money) and a centralized market (quasi-linear utility rebalancing money), so the money distribution stays degenerate and tractable. *Household block:* a two-subperiod composition `DM ∘ CM` — a decentralized-market trade stage `∘` a centralized-market `ConsumptionSavingsStage` over money, the quasi-linear CM resetting holdings. The CM leg **is** `ConsumptionSavingsStage` over money and money-market clearing is genuinely outer-loop; but the DM leg is *not* expressible. Generalized Nash bargaining maximizes a **product** `[u(q)−φd]^θ·[φd−c(q)]^{1−θ}` — not the additive `reward + βV_end` every choice stage optimizes — and it mixes three exogenous role-conditional (buyer/seller/no-match) money maps that no combinator expresses. The dogfooding finding: this is a genuine missing *within-period* primitive, not an outer-loop give-up. *Status:* ✗ — missing within-period primitive (a Nash-bargaining / terms-of-trade stage); the CM leg is `ConsumptionSavingsStage` and money-market clearing is outer-loop, but the DM bargaining stage does not exist.

> **Spine summary.** The large majority are ✅ — exact `∘`/`⊕` of `MarkovStage`, the budget moves, `ConsumptionSavingsStage`, `UtilityStage`/`BorrowingConstraintStage`, and the demographic stages. The honest ◐s share **one** root cause: a *unit-root permanent income component* (Carroll 1997; Constantinides–Duffie 1996; HSV 2009) is not a stationary `MarkovStage` on a fixed grid — these models normalize it away analytically. The sole spine-area ✗ is Lagos–Wright's decentralized-market bargaining leg — a genuine missing within-period primitive (the terms-of-trade / Nash-bargaining stage, recorded under family B of the gaps); every other spine model composes from existing stages.

### §1. Drift / level control — continuous "how much" choices

The household chooses the *level* of a continuous next-period state — how much wealth to carry forward, how large a stock of human capital / health / durables to hold next period. From origin `x` the agent picks a destination `a'` maximizing `reward(x, a') + βV_end(a')` — the `(max,+)` choice over one operative axis. The workhorses are `ContinuousArgmaxStage` (the off-grid primitive: per-cell 1-D maximization, frozen `Float64` policy in an `InterpKernel`) and three domain-named sugars over the grid sibling `ArgmaxStage`: `ConsumptionSavingsStage`, `CapitalInvestmentStage`, `DurableAdjustmentStage`. As coded, all three expand to `ArgmaxStage(reward = to_matrix_source(payoff,…), search = :divide_conquer) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β)`; `ContinuousArgmaxStage` is the off-grid limit used when a genuinely off-grid optimum and a smooth `env`-Jacobian are wanted.

**The convexity boundary.** This is the *smooth/supermodular* case: a convex cost of moving the stock makes the policy monotone, licensing the `:divide_conquer` solve and golden-section. **Non-convex / fixed-cost (S,s) adjustment is a separate family (§5(i))** — where a model has both a smooth interior margin and a lumpy one, the smooth margin is catalogued here and the lumpy one in §5(i). Genuinely non-supermodular continuous choice (reinforcement) routes through the *brute* `ArgmaxStage`.

- **Consumption–savings, the canonical inner choice (Huggett 1993; Aiyagari 1994)** — *mechanism:* given cash-on-hand, choose next-period assets `b'`; `c = b − b'` is the residual, `c ≤ 0` infeasible. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ IncomeStage(:wealth) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth)`. *Fidelity:* exact; the budget constraint is the `typemin` gate, the borrowing limit the grid floor; the β-as-`TimeDiscountingStage` factoring reproduces fused `max(u(c)+βV')` bit-for-bit. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/aiyagari`, `examples/huggett`.
- **Precautionary saving / Bewley–İmrohoroğlu (Bewley 1983; İmrohoroğlu 1989)** — *mechanism:* same savings argmax, the cross-section driven by uninsurable risk + a tight constraint (buffer-stock policy). *Household block:* identical spine. *Fidelity:* the precautionary motive is emergent, not a new primitive. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/bewley`.
- **Wealth-in-utility / "capitalist spirit" (Carroll 2000; Francis 2009)** — *mechanism:* felicity `u(c) + v(b')` — wealth held next period enters utility directly. *Household block:* the canonical block with `ConsumptionSavingsStage(utility = (cell,c;env) -> u(c) + v(cell.wealth_next))`. *Fidelity:* a one-line utility change; the start-and-end reward already sees `b'`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/wealth_in_utility`.
- **Ben-Porath human-capital investment (Ben-Porath 1967)** — *mechanism:* from stock `h` choose `h'`, paying a convex effort cost on gross investment `i = h' − (1−δ)h`, earning `R·h`. *Household block:* `CapitalInvestmentStage(:h; production, effort_cost, depreciation=δ, β)` — a single stage. *Fidelity:* exemplary; the effort cost is the inverted Ben-Porath production function, making the reward supermodular so the monotone solve is exact. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/human_capital`.
- **Life-cycle human capital (Huggett–Ventura–Yaron 2011)** — *mechanism:* Ben-Porath in finite-horizon form with a declining ability profile → hump-shaped earnings. *Household block:* `CapitalInvestmentStage(:h)`, age-specific ability `a_t` threaded through `env`. *Fidelity:* faithful; ability enters as an `env` profile (an ability *axis* would be economically inert). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/human_capital`.
- **Human capital with ability heterogeneity & learning (Guvenen–Kuruşçu 2010)** — *mechanism:* Ben-Porath where households differ in a permanent ability type and a skill-price trend interacts with investment. *Household block:* `⊕_type [ CapitalInvestmentStage(:h) ]`, skill-price path in `env`. *Fidelity:* faithful within-type; ability is the `⊕` axis. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/guvenen_kuruscu` (household block; the rising-inequality result also needs a price *path* / transition driver).
- **Multi-stage human-capital production (Manuelli–Seshadri 2014)** — *mechanism:* human capital produced over a schooling phase + an on-the-job phase, with quality interactions. *Household block:* a `∘`-chain of `CapitalInvestmentStage`-like stages keyed to life phases. *Fidelity:* ◐ — the core investment maps cleanly, but the full-time schooling *corner* is a within-period regime switch handled by the driver. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/manuelli_seshadri`. *Family-pattern note:* the many-phase Lee–Seshadri (2019) skill-formation chain is the *same* household block with a longer `∘`-chain of `CapitalInvestmentStage`s — documented as a pattern extension in this example's README, not separately built (a near-duplicate).
- **Grossman health-capital investment (Grossman 1972)** — *mechanism:* health is a stock yielding a flow and decaying at δ; medical care invests `i = h' − (1−δ)h`; health raises survival. *Household block:* `CapitalInvestmentStage(:health) ∘ MarkovStage(:alive | health)` — investment composed with a health-dependent survival Markov. *Fidelity:* elegant; survival is plain data, the absorbing-death leak handled by a finite-horizon cohort. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/health`.
- **Health spending & the value of life (Hall–Jones 2007)** — *mechanism:* as people get richer, optimal health spending rises as a share of income. *Household block:* the Grossman shape with a value-of-life flow. *Fidelity:* ◐ — the household margin is the Grossman stage; the headline is a calibration result in the flow closures, not a structural feature. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/hall_jones`.
- **Medical-spending risk in old age (De Nardi–French–Jones 2010)** — *mechanism:* retirees save against persistent uninsurable medical *shocks* and longevity risk. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:medical_shock) ∘ MarkovStage(:health) ∘ MarkovStage(:alive|health) ∘ IncomeStage(net of medical) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Fidelity:* the savings inner choice (this family) is exact; the "health investment" reading does not apply — medical spending is a *shock*, not a chosen drift. *Status:* ✅ for the savings choice; catalogued as a Markov-shock model, not a health-investment one.
- **Child human-capital investment (Becker–Tomes 1979, 1986)** — *mechanism:* altruistic parents invest in children's human capital (with bequests), generating intergenerational persistence. *Household block:* `ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth)` `⊕`-coupled to `CapitalInvestmentStage(:h_child)`, with `ReproductionStage` mapping the chosen `h_child'` to the next cohort. *Fidelity:* ◐ — the investment *choice* is clean Ben-Porath; the content is the cross-generation mapping (`ReproductionStage`) + a cohort-iterating driver. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/becker_tomes`. *Family-pattern note:* Caucutt–Lochner (two-phase) and Daruich (early-childhood + public transfers) are the *same* household block with a longer `∘`-chain / more driver structure — documented as pattern extensions in this example's README, not separately built (near-duplicates).
- **Multi-period child HC with credit constraints (Caucutt–Lochner 2020)** — *mechanism:* early- and late-childhood investment are dynamic complements; early constraints depress total investment. *Household block:* two `CapitalInvestmentStage`s (early/late) `∘` the parent's `ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ BorrowingConstraintStage`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — pieces exist; the dynamic complementarity is a multi-period composition. *Status:* ◐ (covered-by-pattern in `examples/becker_tomes`'s README).
- **Dynamic complementarity in skill formation (Lee–Seshadri 2019)** — *mechanism:* human capital produced across many life stages with self-productivity and complementarity. *Household block:* a long `∘`-chain of `CapitalInvestmentStage`s on a `:h` axis, stage-specific profiles in `env`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — "Ben-Porath repeated across life stages"; the bite is in the parameters/horizon. *Status:* ◐ (covered-by-pattern in `examples/manuelli_seshadri`'s README).
- **Early-childhood investment & family policy (Daruich 2022)** — *mechanism:* parental investment in young children with constraints and public transfers, feeding adult human capital. *Household block:* `CapitalInvestmentStage(:h_child) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth) ∘ BorrowingConstraintStage`, closed across generations by `ReproductionStage`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — investment in-family; generational + policy structure is composition/driver. *Status:* ◐ (covered-by-pattern in `examples/becker_tomes`'s README).
- **Smooth durable accumulation (convex adjustment cost)** — *mechanism:* a durable stock `d` yielding a service flow, adjusted continuously under a *convex* cost — the frictionless limit. *Household block:* `DurableAdjustmentStage(:durable; service, adjustment_cost, β)` `∘` optionally `ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth)`. *Fidelity:* exemplary — precisely what `DurableAdjustmentStage` is for. *Status:* ✅. (The *lumpy* (S,s) durable is §5(i).)
- **Smooth business/capital investment (Cooper–Haltiwanger 2006, convex component)** — *mechanism:* choose capital `k'` paying a convex adjustment cost `φ(k'−k)`. *Household block:* `DurableAdjustmentStage(:k; service = π(k';env), adjustment_cost = φ) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth)`. *Fidelity:* ✅ for the convex piece; the non-convex/lumpy component (fixed costs, irreversibility) is §5(i). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/capital_investment` (convex case). *Build note:* the stock uses `CapitalInvestmentStage` (which folds δ into gross investment) since `DurableAdjustmentStage` takes no depreciation kwarg; its reward closure sees only the `:k` axis + `env`, so the idiosyncratic productivity shock enters through a *separable* `UtilityStage` reading that extra axis — which is what gives the non-degenerate capital cross-section. Worked-around closure limitations, not gaps.
- **R&D / intangible-capital investment (textbook / class example)** — *mechanism:* accumulate a knowledge stock `z` via R&D with a convex cost, raising productivity. *Household block:* `CapitalInvestmentStage(:z; production = productivity, effort_cost = convex R&D cost, depreciation = δ_z)`. *Fidelity:* ✅ for the smooth margin; a *discrete* innovation arrival adds a Markov/hazard stage (different family). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/rnd_investment` (convex investment). *Build note:* as in `capital_investment`, the knowledge stock uses `CapitalInvestmentStage` (δ folded into gross investment, since `DurableAdjustmentStage` takes no depreciation kwarg) and the productivity shock enters via a *separable* `UtilityStage` reading the extra axis — worked-around closure limitations, not gaps.
- **Marriage-specific capital accumulation (Becker 1973)** — *mechanism:* a couple invests in match-specific capital raising the surplus of staying together; it decays on separation. *Household block:* `CapitalInvestmentStage(:match_capital) ∘ MarkovStage(:married | match_capital)` (capital-dependent separation hazard). *Fidelity:* ◐ — the within-match investment is clean Ben-Porath + a hazard Markov; the *two-sided* match formation/bargaining is out of this family (matching gap). *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/marriage_capital` (within-match ✅; formation is two-sided).

#### Labor supply — intensive & extensive margins

The most canonical household choice after consumption–savings. Hours are a *level* control (a drift on labor income), so the intensive margin lives in this family; the indivisible/participation margin is a discrete two-state choice that rides `ArgmaxStage`/`LogitChoiceStage` (and the Rogerson lottery `MixingStage`), catalogued here as the labor companion to the savings core.

- **Endogenous-labor Aiyagari / intensive hours (Aiyagari 1994 with endogenous labor; Chang–Kim 2007; Pijoan-Mas 2006; Heathcote–Storesletten–Violante 2014)** — *mechanism:* the household jointly chooses hours `n` and savings `b'`; labor earns `w·n`, hours carry disutility `v(n)`, and `n` feeds the budget that funds savings. *Household block:* a labor `ArgmaxStage` over an hours axis composed with the savings choice — `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:hours; reward = −v(n)) ∘ IncomeStage(:wealth; cash = (1+r)b + w·n) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth)`. *Fidelity:* because `n` and `b'` are *jointly* chosen (hours feed the budget feeding savings), the faithful joint solve is the two-control coupling of §5(ii) — an auxiliary-choice-axis (Route A, as in two-asset HANK): the hours `ArgmaxStage` populates an `:hours` axis, a downstream `WealthChangeStage` reads both `n` and the savings choice, and a `ForgetfulSumStage` collapses the hours axis. That is exact but carries the `n_hours×` auxiliary tensor → ◐. The static intratemporal `n`-FOC `v'(n) = w·u'(c)` folded into the savings reward (a closed-form `n*(b,b')` substituted *before* the savings argmax) collapses the block to the plain spine and is ✅ — but that is an analytic substitution, not a separate stage. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/labor_supply` (joint Route A); ✅ when the intratemporal FOC is closed-form-substituted.
- **Indivisible / extensive-margin labor (Rogerson 1988; Chang–Kim 2007 extensive margin; Prescott–Rogerson–Wallenius 2009)** — *mechanism:* labor is indivisible — a discrete {work, not-work} participation choice — with Rogerson's employment lotteries convexifying the non-convex choice set. *Household block:* the participation choice is an `ArgmaxStage`/`LogitChoiceStage` over a 2-level `:participation` axis (`reward[work] = u(w·n̄) − v(n̄)`, `reward[not] = u(0)`); the Rogerson lottery is a `MixingStage` blending the work-kernel `K_work` and the non-work-kernel `K_not` (`K_θ = θ·K_work + (1−θ)·K_not`, the convexifying employment probability θ). *Fidelity:* exact — the discrete participation is the temperature-0 `ArgmaxStage` (logit-smoothed at `ε>0`), and the convexified lottery is precisely `MixingStage`'s two-kernel blend. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/indivisible_labor` (participation `ArgmaxStage`/`LogitChoiceStage`). *Build note:* with the employment lottery valued linearly and no convex cost, `MixingStage`'s conjugate degenerates to the corner `max(·,·)` — i.e. exactly the discrete participation `ArgmaxStage` (the faithful incomplete-markets Chang–Kim object), so `examples/indivisible_labor` builds the discrete participation `ArgmaxStage`, not a `MixingStage` lottery.

#### Alternative preferences — utility shapes over the savings core

A cluster of models that keep the spine but reshape the felicity: a Pareto-weighted sum (collective), a kink on a reference stock (loss aversion), or a temptation penalty (self-control). Each is a `UtilityStage` shape composed with `ConsumptionSavingsStage` — the economics in the closure, no bespoke stage.

- **Collective / intra-household allocation (Chiappori 1988, 1992; Mazzocco 2007)** — *mechanism:* a couple allocates resources by maximizing a Pareto-weighted sum of the two members' utilities under one budget; with a fixed weight the household acts as a single planner, while limited commitment makes the weight evolve to keep both members participating. *Household block:* `UtilityStage(μ·u_A + (1−μ)·u_B, the intra-period sharing rule in the closure) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage` — the weighted felicity is a one-line `UtilityStage` over the joint savings choice; the *evolving*-weight version adds a `:pareto_weight` axis updated by a participation gate. *Fidelity:* fixed weight is exact (a utility-shape change); the evolving-weight (limited-commitment) version is intricate (a tracked weight axis + per-member participation `−Inf` gate, the Alvarez–Jermann machinery). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/collective_household` (fixed Pareto weight); ◐ (evolving-weight limited commitment, which ties to Alvarez–Jermann, §0).
- **Reference-dependence / loss aversion (Kőszegi–Rabin 2006, 2009)** — *mechanism:* utility is gain–loss over deviations from a reference point, kinked (loss-averse) at the reference, where the reference is a slow-moving stock of recent expectations/consumption. *Household block:* `… ∘ WealthChangeStage(:reference; r' = (1−δ)r + δ·c) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(utility = u(c) + η·μ(c − r), μ kinked at 0)` — a *kinked* `UtilityStage` on a reference stock carried by `WealthChangeStage` (the internal-habit machinery, §5(ii)). *Fidelity:* the kinked felicity on a slow reference stock is exactly the internal-habit composite with a kink at the reference; the personal-equilibrium consistency (reference = rational expectation of own behavior) is the caller's outer loop. *Status:* ✅ (the gain–loss felicity) / ◐ (the slow reference stock is the §5(ii) habit composite).
- **Temptation & self-control (Gul–Pesendorfer 2001)** — *mechanism:* the ranking is `u(c) + w(c) − max_{c̃∈B} w(c̃)`: a self-control disutility equal to the foregone temptation utility of the most-tempting feasible option — and, crucially, the preference is **time-consistent** (unlike present-bias, a genuine gap, A2). *Household block:* a temptation `ArgmaxStage(reward = w)` over the *shared* feasible set feeding a `UtilityStage(− max_{c̃} w)` shifter `∘` the commitment `ArgmaxStage(reward = u + w)` — two argmaxes over one feasible set, the temptation `max` subtracted as an additive shifter. *Fidelity:* the two-extrema-over-a-shared-set structure is expressible (temptation `ArgmaxStage` → `UtilityStage` shifter → commitment `ArgmaxStage`); intricate because both extrema read the same budget set. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/temptation` (the static self-control block) / ◐ (the shared-feasible-set coupling). Time-consistency is exactly what keeps it inside the stage vocabulary, unlike Laibson present-bias (gap A2).

> **§1 summary.** Shipped: consumption–savings (`aiyagari`/`huggett`/`bewley`), Ben-Porath (`human_capital`), Grossman health (`health`), the smooth-durable margin (`durable_housing`), wealth-in-utility (`wealth_in_utility`), Guvenen–Kuruşçu (`guvenen_kuruscu`), smooth capital and R&D (`capital_investment`/`rnd_investment`), Hall–Jones (`hall_jones`), labor supply on both margins (`labor_supply`/`indivisible_labor`), the alternative-preference cluster (`collective_household`/`temptation`), and the child/multi-stage human-capital and marriage-capital ◐s (`becker_tomes`/`manuelli_seshadri`/`marriage_capital`). The ◐s all need a cross-period or cross-generation composition (`ReproductionStage`, multi-phase chains, capital-dependent hazards) — none a new primitive. The recurring boundary is **convex vs. non-convex cost**: every fixed-cost/irreversible margin is deferred to §5(i), where the adjust-value is an inner continuous argmax of *this* family nested inside a discrete keep/adjust choice.

### §2. Dispersion / variance control — RI, portfolio, risk-taking, robustness

The household chooses not *where* the state lands but how *spread out* the transition is — the variance/risk exposure of next-period outcomes, the precision of a signal, or (robustness) a min over distortions. The workhorses are `MeanVarianceStage` (chosen risky share, over `MeanVarianceKernel`) and `ScaleVarianceStage` (chosen dispersion of a mean-preserving spread, over `MPSKernel`) — both streaming, `O(n_x)`, per-cell float policy `θ*(x)`, **no auxiliary choice axis** — together with `MixingStage`/`RetentionStage` (the closed-form conjugate blend) and `LogitChoiceStage` at **ε<0** (the soft-MIN / entropic-risk member). The unifying fact: insurance and gambling are *one stage on opposite sides of the continuation's curvature* (concave V ⇒ insure, θ↓; convex V ⇒ gamble, θ↑), and variance-RI is that same stage with an information cost.

#### Portfolio choice — chosen risky share (`MeanVarianceStage`)
- **Merton (1969) / Samuelson (1969)** — *mechanism:* split savings between safe and risky; share θ raises mean and variance, a CRRA agent picks an interior θ. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MeanVarianceStage`. *Fidelity:* elegant — four stages, no bespoke; the continuous-time constant-share is the small-grid limit. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/portfolio`.
- **Cocco–Gomes–Maenhout (2005)** — *mechanism:* life-cycle portfolio with non-tradable labor income tilting the young toward equities. *Household block:* `replicate_age(IncomeShock ∘ Receipt ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MeanVarianceStage, N; axis=:age)`. *Fidelity:* faithful; the age profile of θ* from per-age calibration. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/cocco_gomes_maenhout`.
- **Catherine (2022)** — *mechanism:* countercyclical (left-skewed) idiosyncratic risk makes the human-wealth hedge state-dependent. *Household block:* the life-cycle chain with cyclical income `MarkovStage` and `MeanVarianceStage` returns via `FromEnv`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/catherine`.
- **Gomes–Michaelides (2005)** — *mechanism:* heterogeneous preferences + a fixed participation cost matching participation and shares. *Household block:* `⊕`-over-preference-type of the Merton chain, the participation cost in `MeanVarianceStage`'s `cost` vector (`cost[θ=0]=0`, `cost[θ>0]=F`). *Fidelity:* the discrete participation margin rides the streaming primitive's `cost`. *Dogfooding note:* `MeanVarianceStage`'s `cost(θ; env)` sees only θ+env (not the cell), so the cost is an additive *utils* penalty — Gomes–Michaelides's literal *monetary* fixed participation cost isn't expressible and the participation–wealth gradient is sign-correct only for σ<1 (the example calibrates that). A worked-around closure limitation, not a gap. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/gomes_michaelides`.
- **Calvet–Campbell–Sodini (2007)** — *mechanism:* empirical rebalancing; benchmark is a mean-variance/CRRA portfolio with inertia. *Household block:* the Merton chain; inertia a `MixingStage` blending "hold" vs "re-optimize". *Fidelity:* ◐ — benchmark ✅; the paper's contribution is measurement. *Status:* ◐.
- **Fagereng–Guiso–Malacrino–Pistaferri (2020)** — *mechanism:* persistent *heterogeneity in returns to wealth* (a fat-tail driver). *Household block:* `ReturnType(MarkovStage :rtype) ∘ Receipt ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MeanVarianceStage`, return state feeding returns via `FromEnv`. *Fidelity:* faithful — "the return process is the model's, not the package's". *Dogfooding note:* `MeanVarianceStage`'s risky returns are scalars/vectors, not axis-closures, so a return can't be gated by a productivity/occupation axis — which is why the return heterogeneity rides the `WealthChangeStage`/`FromEnv` return-state dep here (faithful: the return process is the model's). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/fagereng`.
- **Campbell–Viceira (2002), strategic allocation** — *mechanism:* multi-period mean-variance with predictable returns and hedging demand. *Household block:* `ReturnState(MarkovStage) ∘ Receipt ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MeanVarianceStage`. *Status:* ✅ (covered by `examples/fagereng` — the same return-state-Markov + `MeanVarianceStage` block).
- **Two-asset HANK, liquid/illiquid portfolio (Kaplan–Moll–Violante 2018)** — *mechanism:* a low-return liquid + high-return illiquid asset, rebalanced under a convex adjustment cost. *Household block:* the portfolio-value reformulation (§5(ii)): `… ∘ Rebalance(:illiquid ContinuousArgmaxStage) ∘ Consume(:portfolio)`. *Fidelity:* elegant — `examples/two_asset_hank_pv` is bit-exact vs the brute `(W,a)` Bellman. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/two_asset_hank_pv`. (The illiquid choice here is a *level*; a risky-return version composes `MeanVarianceStage` on the illiquid leg.)
- **CARA–normal portfolio benchmark (textbook)** — *mechanism:* the textbook `θ* = (μ−R_f)/(γσ²)`, wealth-independent. *Household block:* `MeanVarianceStage(axis=:wealth, shares, risk_free, risky_returns, probs)`. *Fidelity:* the streaming `(max,+)` recovers θ* up to discretization. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/cara_normal` (the textbook check on `MeanVarianceStage`).

#### Gaussian / variance rational inattention (`ScaleVarianceStage`)
- **Sims (2003), LQG rational inattention** — *mechanism:* choose signal precision at λ·(Shannon mutual information); coarser attention leaves a more dispersed effective state. *Household block:* `… ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ Attention(ScaleVarianceStage, cost = (θ;env) -> λ·KL(θ))`. *Fidelity:* the per-period precision choice is faithful (the KL-of-θ cost is a legitimate `cost` closure). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/rational_inattention`; ◐ for the *full recursive belief-state LQG* (the prior variance would be a tracked axis).
- **Luo (2008), RI consumption** — *mechanism:* permanent-income consumption with an information-flow constraint, raising own-past sensitivity. *Household block:* `… ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ ScaleVarianceStage(cost = λ·info(θ))`. *Status:* ✅ (covered by `examples/rational_inattention` — the same `ScaleVarianceStage` block; ◐ on the recursive-belief caveat).
- **Maćkowiak–Wiederholt (2009, 2015)** — *mechanism:* allocate finite attention across aggregate vs idiosyncratic conditions. *Household block:* `ScaleVarianceStage` (idiosyncratic attention) `∘` an aggregate `MarkovStage`. *Fidelity:* the single-margin household is faithful; the *attention budget across multiple signals* couples precisions through one constraint. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/mackowiak_wiederholt` (the single-attention-margin build — per-axis ✅; the multi-signal attention budget / cross-signal coupling stays the recorded ◐/G3-adjacent gap).
- **Kamdar (2019); Zorn, RI consumption-savings** — *mechanism:* attention to income news governs savings responsiveness. *Household block:* `… ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ ScaleVarianceStage(cost = λ·info(θ))`. *Status:* ✅ (covered by `examples/rational_inattention` — the same `ScaleVarianceStage` block; ◐ on recursive belief dynamics).

## Risk-taking & entrepreneurial investment risk (convex/floored V)
- **Vereshchagina–Hopenhayn (2009), gambling for resurrection** — *mechanism:* a constrained, limited-liability entrepreneur whose continuation is convex near the floor takes more risk at no higher mean. *Household block:* `… ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ Gamble(MeanVarianceStage, mean-neutral two-point) ∘ LimitedLiability(WealthChangeStage, a↦max(z·a, a_floor))`. *Fidelity:* elegant — the floor convexifies V and a *mean-neutral* gamble makes convexity drive risk-taking, no skewness primitive. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/risk_shifting`.
- **Uninsured idiosyncratic investment risk (Angeletos 2007; Covas 2006)** — *mechanism:* undiversifiable idiosyncratic capital-return risk adds a precautionary wedge to investment. *Household block:* `… ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MeanVarianceStage(axis=:capital, idiosyncratic return)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/uninsured_investment_risk`.
- **Entrepreneurship & wealth inequality (Quadrini 2000; Cagetti–De Nardi 2006)** — *mechanism:* an occupational choice worker vs (risky, constrained) entrepreneur. *Household block:* `⊕`-over-`:occupation` of {worker: spine} and {entrepreneur: `… ∘ MeanVarianceStage(business risk)`}, the leg chosen by `LogitChoiceStage`/`ArgmaxStage`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — the *risk* margin is clean `MeanVarianceStage`, but applying distinct savings problems to one leg is per-axis gating, clean only as a wealth-floor or a fully separate `⊕` leg. *Dogfooding note:* `MeanVarianceStage`'s risky returns are scalars/vectors, not axis-closures, so there is no span-of-control return gating on a productivity/occupation axis — the structural reason this stays ◐; built via the separate-`⊕`-leg form, where the literal `product` of different-length chains is the documented give-up. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/entrepreneurship` (cross-ref §4 for the occupational choice).

#### Insurance / annuitization / retention (`MixingStage`/`RetentionStage`)
- **Yaari (1965) annuities / standard insurance demand** — *mechanism:* pay a convex premium for coverage θ blending toward a safe transition. *Household block:* `IncomeShock ∘ Insurance(MixingStage: K_A=I, K_B=loss, c(θ)=θ²/2κ) ∘ Receipt ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Fidelity:* elegant — closed form `V = K_B·V + c*(K_A·V − K_B·V)`, two applies + a Fenchel conjugate, no θ-axis. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/insurance`.
- **Grossman-type mortality/health retention** — *mechanism:* "pay convex cost *not* to transition" — stay alive/employed. *Household block:* `RetentionStage(axis, exit_kernel, cost_curvature)` = `MixingStage` with `K_A=I`. *Status:* ✅.

#### Robustness & ambiguity aversion (`LogitChoiceStage` at ε<0)
- **Hansen–Sargent (2008) multiplier prefs; Hansen–Sargent–Tallarini (1999)** — *mechanism:* a min over entropy-penalized distortions → the exponential/entropic certainty equivalent (pessimistic tilt). *Household block:* the continuation aggregator is `LogitChoiceStage` at **ε<0**: `V = ε·log Σ exp((−C+V_end)/ε)` is the soft-MIN, the multiplier `|ε|`. *Fidelity:* faithful — robustness "comes for free" from the entropy member at flipped sign. *Dogfooding note:* `LogitChoiceStage.forward!` pushes Λ through the *seated worst-case* kernel, so the value distortion and the simulated measure are coupled in one stage — a caveat for a robust-control exercise wanting value-under-worst-case but distribution-under-reference, which one stage can't separate. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/hansen_sargent` (the ε<0 build).
- **Ilut–Schneider (2014), ambiguous business cycles** — *mechanism:* act on a worst-case mean from an ambiguity set (max-min). *Household block:* the smoothed version is `LogitChoiceStage` at ε<0 over the distortion set; the literal max-min is the ε→0⁻ limit. *Dogfooding note:* `LogitChoiceStage.forward!` pushing Λ through the seated worst-case kernel couples value distortion and the simulated measure in one stage — here desirable, since that coupling *is* Ilut–Schneider's amplification. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/ilut_schneider` (entropic form ✅; exact set-based max-min is limiting).
- **Klibanoff–Marinacci–Mukerji (2005), smooth ambiguity** — *mechanism:* a *second-order* expectation `E_prior φ(E[u|prior])`, φ concave. *Household block:* with *exponential* φ this collapses to multiplier prefs (= ε<0); a general φ needs a nested nonlinear aggregator over a prior axis. *Status:* ◐ for exponential φ; ✗ for general φ (a missing nonlinear second-order-expectation aggregator, adjacent to G3).

> **§2 summary.** Example-backed core: `MeanVarianceStage` (`portfolio`, `cara_normal`, life-cycle portfolio `cocco_gomes_maenhout`/`catherine`, the participation-cost `gomes_michaelides`, heterogeneous returns `fagereng`, uninsured investment risk `uninsured_investment_risk`, risk-shifting `risk_shifting`), `ScaleVarianceStage` (variance RI `rational_inattention`; the single-margin `mackowiak_wiederholt`), `MixingStage`/`RetentionStage` (`insurance`, retention), `LogitChoiceStage` ε<0 (robustness `hansen_sargent`/`ilut_schneider`). Three reward-closure limitations the dogfooding surfaced — all worked around, none a bug: (a) `MeanVarianceStage`'s risky returns are scalars/vectors, not axis-closures (no productivity/occupation gating of a return — keeps entrepreneurship ◐, rides a `WealthChangeStage`/`FromEnv` dep in `fagereng`); (b) `MeanVariance`/`ScaleVariance` `cost(θ; env)` sees only θ+env, not the cell (cost is an additive *utils* penalty, so Gomes–Michaelides's *monetary* participation cost isn't literal and the participation–wealth gradient is sign-correct only for σ<1); (c) `LogitChoiceStage.forward!` pushes Λ through the seated worst-case kernel (value distortion and simulated measure coupled in one stage — desirable for Ilut–Schneider, a caveat for a robust-control exercise). The remaining ◐/✗ edges cluster on (i) G3-adjacent non-entropy/coupled convex costs (multi-signal attention; general perturbed-utility) and (ii) per-leg gating of distinct sub-problems (entrepreneurial occupation), clean only as a wealth-floor or separate `⊕` leg. KMM with non-exponential φ is the one genuine ✗ (a missing nonlinear aggregator).

### §3. Hazard / probability control — search, effort, survival, discrete attention

The household scales the probability of reaching a target state — finding a job, staying alive, escaping a bad shock, attending to one action — at a convex effort, search, or information cost. Three members realize the family: `SearchMatchingStage` (effort to raise the hazard of a target transition, with an optional aggregate-tightness externality), `RetentionStage` (the `K_A=I` member: pay *not* to undergo a transition), and `LogitChoiceStage` (the entropy member: a posterior over discrete actions at a Shannon cost — the discrete-RI foundation). `SearchMatchingStage`/`RetentionStage` carry the policy as a per-cell field (no auxiliary axis); `LogitChoiceStage` computes the log-sum-exp directly. Retention and search are the two directions of one hazard control — which transition you subsidize.

#### Search & matching (`SearchMatchingStage`)
- **McCall job search (McCall 1970; Mortensen 1970)** — *mechanism:* the unemployed choose search effort `e` at convex disutility, finding a job with prob `p(e)`; employed separate at `δ`. *Household block:* `SearchMatchingStage(:emp)`; with wage *dispersion* (offers on a `:wage` axis) the accept margin is instead an `ArgmaxStage`/`LogitChoiceStage` over offers `∘` an offer-draw `MarkovStage`. *Fidelity:* faithful — the stage is purpose-built for the effort-max-plus-Bernoulli-matching recursion (reservation-wage logic is the single-effort special case). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/mccall_search` (the reservation-wage build).
- **Search intensity / endogenous effort (Mortensen 1970)** — *mechanism:* the job-finding rate `p(e,θ)` rises in own effort and tightness. *Household block:* `SearchMatchingStage` with a multi-point effort grid and a general convex cost. *Fidelity:* faithful — the dedicated stage exists precisely because effort wants the effort axis alive in opposite temporal positions on forward/backward (no linear `∘` chain supplies it). *Status:* ✅ (and this resolves the "◐ for general convex cost ≠ entropy" caveat: a general convex *effort* cost is handled exactly by the hard-max-over-grid; the entropy restriction applies only to the `LogitChoiceStage` route).
- **Mortensen–Pissarides matching with a tightness externality (Mortensen–Pissarides 1994; Pissarides 2000)** — *mechanism:* every worker's job-finding rate depends on aggregate tightness `θ`, pinned by firms' free entry. *Household block:* `SearchMatchingStage ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`, `tightness = FromEnv(:θ)`; `κ = q(θ)·J` closes `θ` in the driver. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/search_matching`.
- **Search + savings / self-insurance (Krusell–Mukoyama–Şahin 2010)** — *mechanism:* risk-averse workers save while searching, so the margins interact through wealth. *Household block:* `SearchMatchingStage ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/search_matching`.
- **On-the-job search / wage-ladder climbing (Burdett–Mortensen 1998; Postel-Vinay–Robin 2002; Lise 2013)** — *mechanism:* the employed climb a wage ladder at an effort/offer-controlled hazard; the wage distribution is an equilibrium object. *Household block:* a multi-rung wage axis with an effort-controlled *upward* hazard — a `MixingStage`/`KernelChoiceStage` blending stay-rung vs climb-rung at convex cost, `∘` the separation `MarkovStage`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — `SearchMatchingStage` is strictly two-level (`@assert n_l==2`), so the ladder is a composition, not the single stage; the offer distribution is an outer-loop fixed point. *Status:* ◐ — the *exogenous* effort-controlled rung ladder is shipped as `examples/promotion_ladder` (a `MixingStage`/`MarkovStage` rung composition); only the *equilibrium* offer distribution (the Burdett–Mortensen fixed point) stays outer-loop.
- **Endogenous separations / quits (Mortensen–Pissarides 1994, separations leg)** — *mechanism:* the employed choose whether to keep the match as quality evolves. *Household block:* a `RetentionStage` on the employed sub-mass (`K_A=I` vs separation kernel), or an `ArgmaxStage`/`LogitChoiceStage` over {keep, quit}, `∘` the inflow `SearchMatchingStage`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/endogenous_separations` (the keep/quit retention composition).
- **Skill depreciation while unemployed (Ljungqvist–Sargent 1998; Pissarides 1992)** — *mechanism:* human capital decays during unemployment, sharpening search incentives. *Household block:* `SearchMatchingStage ∘ MarkovStage(:skill | emp)` (employment-dependent decay). *Fidelity:* faithful — the decay is an employment-stratified `MarkovStage`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/skill_depreciation`.
- **Promotion / career effort (textbook / class example)** — *mechanism:* pay convex effort to raise the promotion hazard up a job ladder. *Household block:* `ArgmaxStage`/`LogitChoiceStage` over effort `∘ MarkovStage` over rungs, or a `MixingStage` for two rungs. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/promotion_ladder` (the *exogenous* effort-controlled rung ladder; the equilibrium on-the-job-search offer distribution above is the outer-loop fixed point).

#### Survival, mortality & retention (`RetentionStage` / sub-stochastic `MarkovStage`)
- **Exogenous mortality / survival (Grossman 1972; Yaari 1965)** — *mechanism:* survive with prob `s` (health/age-dependent), dead absorbing; mass leaks. *Household block:* a sub-stochastic `MarkovStage(:alive | health, age)`, or `ExogenousExit` when death carries a bequest (`V_start = s·V_end + (1−s)·bequest`). *Status:* ✅ (the mortality leg of `examples/health`; the bequest version is `ExogenousExit`).
- **Survival/longevity effort (Grossman survival; Pijoan-Mas–Ríos-Rull 2014)** — *mechanism:* pay a convex cost to *raise* survival (avoid death). *Household block:* `RetentionStage(:alive; exit_kernel = death_kernel, cost_curvature)`. *Fidelity:* "pay not to transition" is exactly `RetentionStage`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/longevity_effort`. *Build notes:* (a) `RetentionStage`'s survival cost is a *utils* penalty (the Fenchel conjugate), not a resource drain — a model wanting survival effort paid out of the budget uses `CapitalInvestmentStage` instead (cf. `examples/health`); (b) `ConsumptionSavingsStage` needs an explicit `utility_axes` so its utility closure can read the non-operative `:alive` axis (used here to zero dead-state utility). (The health-*capital*-investment leg is `CapitalInvestmentStage(:health) ∘ MarkovStage(:alive|health)`, §1.)
- **Health-capital investment with mortality (Grossman 1972)** — see §1 (`examples/health`); listed here for the survival hazard. *Status:* ✅ 🏗.
- **Prevention / self-protection effort (Ehrlich–Becker 1972)** — *mechanism:* pay convex effort to *lower* the probability of a bad shock (self-protection). *Household block:* `RetentionStage(:axis; exit_kernel = bad_shock_kernel)`. *Status:* ✅ (covered by `examples/longevity_effort` — the *same* `RetentionStage`, swapping the exit kernel from death to the bad shock; not separately built).
- **Insurance with a convex premium (Yaari 1965; loss-insurance demand)** — see §2 (`examples/insurance`); the retention reading of coverage. *Status:* ✅ 🏗.
- **Default *hazard* — soft probability of default** — *mechanism:* scale the *probability* of default (a smooth hazard) at a convex cost. *Household block:* `MixingStage`/`RetentionStage` blending repay-continue vs default-reset, or `LogitEndogenousExit` (`p_stay = e^{V/ε}/(e^{V/ε}+e^{b/ε})`). *Fidelity:* the soft reading; the **hard discrete-stopping** default is §5(i) (`DefaultStage`). The soft-hazard and hard-jump are the ε→0 endpoints of one control. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/soft_default`.

#### Discrete rational inattention & the entropy member (`LogitChoiceStage`)
- **Discrete RI choice (Matějka–McKay 2015)** — *mechanism:* choose a posterior over discrete actions at Shannon cost λ; the optimal posterior `P(a'|z) ∝ q(a')·exp((flow+V)/λ)` *is* a generalized logit. *Household block:* `LogitUtilityStage(:occupation, ε=λ) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage ∘ MarkovStage(:income | occupation)`, where `LogitUtilityStage = LogitChoiceStage(ε=λ) ∘ UtilityStage(λ·log q + flow)`. *Fidelity:* elegant — the RI posterior falls out because the same λ is the logit temperature and the log-prior weight. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/discrete_ri`. The endogenous prior `q` is the Matějka–McKay consistency condition, converged in the outer loop.
- **Dynamic discrete RI (Steiner–Stewart–Matějka 2017)** — *mechanism:* the static-RI logit extended to a dynamic environment where the action shapes the continuation. *Household block:* the same chain — the action-dependent `MarkovStage` makes the choice dynamic. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 (the dynamic structure of `examples/discrete_ri`).
- **RI discrete-choice demand (Matějka–McKay 2015, demand reading)** — *mechanism:* the RI-microfounded multinomial logit, attention prior shifting shares. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage(ε=λ) ∘ UtilityStage(λ·log q + payoff)` over a `:product` axis. *Status:* ✅ (covered by `examples/discrete_ri` — the *same* `LogitChoiceStage`-at-`ε=λ` entropy block read as demand; not separately built).
- **General posterior-separable / non-Shannon RI (Caplin–Dean 2015; Caplin–Dean–Leahy 2019)** — *mechanism:* attention costs *not* the Shannon information yield posteriors departing from the logit (consideration sets, zero-probability actions). *Status:* ◐/✗ — the **G3** gap (`LogitChoiceStage` covers only the entropy cost). Shannon RI ✅; non-Shannon RI missing.
- **Gaussian / variance RI (Sims 2003; Maćkowiak–Wiederholt 2009)** — the continuous-precision sibling; see §2 (`examples/rational_inattention`). *Status:* ✅ 🏗. (Discrete-posterior RI = entropy/`LogitChoiceStage`; continuous-precision RI = KL/`ScaleVarianceStage`.)
- **Robustness / risk-sensitivity (Hansen–Sargent 2008)** — `LogitChoiceStage` at ε<0; see §2. *Status:* ✅.

#### Optimal-contract boundary (positive block ✅, planner out of scope)
- **Optimal UI with moral hazard (Hopenhayn–Nicolini 1997; Shavell–Weiss 1979)** — *mechanism:* a planner designs benefits trading insurance vs search-effort moral hazard, using promised utility as a state. *Household block:* the *positive* worker side (effort responding to the benefit) is a `SearchMatchingStage` (covered by `examples/search_matching`). *Status:* ✅ for the positive worker block; the *optimal contract* (a planner's promised-utility recursion) is an outer-loop/planner problem — out of scope for a household-stages package, like market clearing, not a household-stage gap.

> **§3 summary.** Example-backed: McCall reservation-wage search (`mccall_search`), Mortensen–Pissarides search+savings (`search_matching`), endogenous separations/quits (`endogenous_separations`), skill depreciation while unemployed (`skill_depreciation`), promotion/career effort (`promotion_ladder`), survival/longevity effort (`longevity_effort`), soft default hazard (`soft_default`), insurance/retention (`insurance`), Grossman health (`health`), discrete RI (`discrete_ri`), Gaussian RI (`rational_inattention`) — Ehrlich–Becker prevention covered by `longevity_effort` (swap the exit kernel) and the RI demand reading by `discrete_ri`. Faithful by composition: search intensity, robustness (ε<0, §2). *Ergonomic note (not a bug):* `SearchMatchingStage` exposes no `policy()` accessor — the effort policy is read off the kernel field. Genuine household-stage gap here: non-Shannon RI (◐/✗ — G3). On-the-job wage ladders are a composition (◐, not a gap); optimal UI *contracts* are a planner/outer-loop problem (out of scope, not a household-stage gap). Recurring boundary: the *household's* hazard/effort is a stage; the *equilibrium/planner object* it feeds (θ, the offer distribution, the prior q, the optimal benefit) is the outer loop.

### §4. Direction / support control — migration, directed search, occupational & matching choice

The household chooses **where to aim** a discrete move — which location, submarket, sector, occupation, partner, school, or neighborhood — over a destination axis, paying an origin→destination cost. Every member is the same object: a soft (Gumbel-logit) or hard (tropical) choice over a destination axis, with the move cost in the kernel and each destination's *value* supplied by stages composed **after** the move. The workhorses are thin sugar over `LogitChoiceStage` (`MigrationStage`/`DirectedSearchStage`/`SectorSwitchingStage` are literally this with a domain name and default axis; `ε→0` the hard limit, `ε>0` Menzio–Shi/Choo–Siow, `ε<0` robust) and `ArgmaxStage` (deterministic Roy selection). **One-sided choice is a stage; the two-sided equilibrium is the outer loop** — when both sides choose and a market clears, that fixed point is the caller's outer loop (as for every GE here), so it is noted, never scored against the block.

#### Spatial migration & regional labor
- **Caliendo–Dvorkin–Parro (2019)** — *mechanism:* forward-looking workers choose a region-sector under Gumbel shocks, paying a bilateral moving cost — the canonical dynamic-discrete-choice migration whose value recursion *is* the logit log-sum-exp. *Household block:* `UtilityStage(real wage + amenity) ∘ MigrationStage(:market; migration_cost, ε=ν) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β)`. *Fidelity:* faithful — the cost in `cost_matrix`, dispersion `ν` as `ε`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/spatial` (a miniature two-location spatial-Aiyagari demonstrator — `MigrationStage` over `{:home,:abroad}` — not the full region-sector model, but the same logit-migration block).
- **Kennan–Walker (2011)** — *mechanism:* location choice for expected income, with a moving cost and *Bayesian learning* about location-specific match values. *Household block:* `MigrationStage(:location) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ DeterministicContinuousStage(belief update) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β)`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — the destination choice is a clean `MigrationStage`/`LogitChoiceStage`, but the Bayesian *active-learning* update is not a straight composition: the signal distribution is choice-dependent and, under anticipated utility, the subjective backward kernel differs from the objective forward kernel (the NEW GAP, family C in the gaps section). *Status:* ◐ — destination sub-block clean; only the active learning is blocked.
- **Monte–Redding–Rossi-Hansberg (2018)** — *mechanism:* a gravity/logit commuting choice with bilateral commuting costs. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage(:workplace; cost_matrix = commute_cost, ε) ∘ UtilityStage(wage − rent + amenity)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/monte_redding_rrh` (the commuting block); wages/rents/measure clear in the caller's spatial GE (outer loop, as always).
- **Bryan–Morten (2019)** — *mechanism:* internal migration with fixed+variable moving costs, generating selection on ability. *Household block:* `MigrationStage(:location; migration_cost = fixed + variable, ε) ∘ UtilityStage(location wage × ability)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/bryan_morten`.
- **Tombe–Zhu (2019)** — *mechanism:* migration + trade over region-sector cells. *Household block:* `MigrationStage(:region×sector; migration_cost, ε) ∘ UtilityStage(real wage)` over a composite axis. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/tombe_zhu` (the migration block; the trade GE is outer-loop).
- **Diamond (2016)** — *mechanism:* skill-specific workers choose a city (wages/rents/amenities), amenities endogenous to skill composition. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage(:city) ∘ UtilityStage(wage − rent + amenity(skill))`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/diamond` (the city-choice block); endogenous amenities + the wage/rent equilibrium are the caller's outer loop.

#### Directed & competitive search
- **Moen (1997)** — *mechanism:* workers aim at a submarket indexed by a posted wage and queue/tightness, trading wages vs fill probability. *Household block:* `DirectedSearchStage(:submarket; search_cost, ε) ∘ MarkovStage(:employment; fill-prob) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 (`examples/directed_search`); the tightness schedule `θ(w)` from free entry is outer-loop.
- **Menzio–Shi (2011)** — *mechanism:* directed search on/off the job; *block recursivity* makes the value functions distribution-independent. *Household block:* `DirectedSearchStage(:submarket) ∘ MarkovStage(:employment) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Fidelity:* elegant — block recursivity is exactly the condition that makes this a clean stage with no distribution-as-state dependence. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/menzio_shi` (on/off-the-job directed search). (The Wright–Kircher et al. directed-search class all maps to `DirectedSearchStage`.)
- **Mortensen–Pissarides (boundary; cross-ref §3)** — the hazard-controlled sibling: the "destination" is a binary employment transition via a matching function; the worker chooses *effort*, not a free destination → `SearchMatchingStage` (`examples/search_matching`). *Status:* ✅ 🏗.

#### Occupational & sectoral choice
- **Roy (1951)** — *mechanism:* self-selection across sectors by comparative advantage — take the highest-paying sector given a skill draw. *Household block:* `ArgmaxStage(:sector; reward = sector earnings) ∘ WealthChangeStage` (Gumbel-Roy uses `SectorSwitchingStage`/`LogitChoiceStage`). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/sectoral` (the *dynamic* Roy with switching costs — `SectorSwitchingStage` over `{:ag,:mfg,:svc}` — citing Artuç–Chaudhuri–McLaren 2010, Dix-Carneiro 2014).
- **Lee–Wolpin (2006)** — *mechanism:* dynamic sectoral choice with sector-specific human capital and switching costs. *Household block:* `SectorSwitchingStage(:sector; switching_cost, ε) ∘ MarkovStage/CapitalInvestmentStage(experience) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ TimeDiscountingStage`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — composable *in principle* (every leg is an existing stage); the ◐ is a per-sector-experience-axis state-space blow-up, **not** a feasibility gap. *Status:* ◐ (dimensional, not a missing primitive).
- **Hsieh–Hurst–Jones–Klenow (2019)** — *mechanism:* talent allocation across occupations under Gumbel preferences and discriminatory wedges `τ`. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage(:occupation; cost_matrix = τ-wedges, ε) ∘ UtilityStage(occupation wage × talent)`. *Fidelity:* faithful — wedges drop into `cost_matrix` (group-varying via `FromEnv`), Gumbel dispersion into `ε`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/hsieh_hurst_jones_klenow` (occupation wages clear in the outer loop).
- **Sectoral reallocation & unemployment (Dvorkin)** — *mechanism:* dynamic discrete sector choice with Gumbel shocks and moving costs. *Household block:* `SectorSwitchingStage(:sector) ∘ MarkovStage(:employment) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅ (exact paper cite uncertain — Dvorkin's sectoral-shocks/reallocation work; the structure maps regardless).
- **Artuç–Chaudhuri–McLaren (2010)** — *mechanism:* trade-shock labor reallocation — workers reallocate across sectors after a trade shock, paying idiosyncratic Gumbel-distributed switching costs, so adjustment is gradual and the option value of moving matters. *Household block:* `SectorSwitchingStage(:sector; switching_cost, ε) ∘ MarkovStage(:prod) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage` — the dynamic-Roy block, with the trade shock entering sector wages via `env`. *Fidelity:* faithful — the switching cost is the `cost_matrix`, the Gumbel dispersion is `ε`; the gross-flows/option-value content is exactly what the logit switch supplies. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/sectoral` (the `examples/sectoral` demonstrator cites ACM 2010 and Dix-Carneiro 2014 directly).
- **Entrepreneurship-vs-work (Cagetti–De Nardi 2006; cross-ref §2)** — the occupational *choice* is direction-control sugar; the entrepreneur branch's risky technology is a §2 `MeanVarianceStage` leaf composed via `⊕`. *Status:* ✅ for the discrete choice.

#### Marriage & partner matching
- **Choo–Siow (2006)** — *mechanism:* a frictionless TU marriage market whose one-sided matching probabilities are exactly a multinomial logit over partner types. *Household block:* `UtilityStage(flow by marital state) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β) ∘ LogitChoiceStage(:marital; cost from match payoffs Π, ε) ∘ MarkovStage(:marital; dissolution hazard)`. *Fidelity:* the cleanest possible fit — the Choo–Siow probabilities *are* the logit. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/choo_siow` (one-sided, verified).
- **Galichon–Salanié (TU matching)** — *mechanism:* two-sided assignment with transfers — both sides choose, the matching distribution + transfers solve a coupled fixed point. *Status:* ✅ for each side's household block (the per-side accept *is* a `LogitChoiceStage`); the two-sided assignment that couples them is an equilibrium fixed point for the caller's outer loop — the same kind of object as market clearing, not a household-stage gap.
- **Greenwood–Guner–Kocharkov–Santos (2014)** — *mechanism:* assortative mating ("Marry Your Like", AER P&P) and its feedback to inequality. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage/ArgmaxStage(:partner_type; accept/reject) ∘ MarkovStage(:marital) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ◐ for the one-sided accept/reject household block; the two-sided sorting equilibrium is the caller's outer loop (not a household-stage gap).

#### Schooling, residential sorting & adoption
- **Arcidiacono (2004)** — *mechanism:* dynamic college/major choice under Gumbel shocks with *learning about own ability*. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage(:major) ∘ DeterministicContinuousStage(belief update) ∘ WealthChangeStage(earnings/tuition) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β)`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — like Kennan–Walker, the `:major` choice is a clean `LogitChoiceStage`, but the Bayesian active-learning about own ability is blocked by the subjective-backward ≠ objective-forward kernel issue (the NEW GAP, family C). *Status:* ◐ — choice clean; the learning is the blocked piece.
- **Bayer–Ferreira–McMillan (2007)** — *mechanism:* an equilibrium sorting model — households choose a neighborhood via a (mixed) logit; prices clear each submarket. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage(:neighborhood) ∘ UtilityStage(amenities + composition − price)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/bayer_ferreira_mcmillan` (the neighborhood-choice block); the clearing price vector is the caller's outer loop.
- **Technology / network adoption** — *mechanism:* adopt when the payoff (depending on others' adoption share) exceeds the cost. *Household block:* `LogitChoiceStage/ArgmaxStage(:technology ∈ {adopt, not}) ∘ UtilityStage(payoff(adoption share))`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/technology_adoption` (the adoption-choice block); the network externality (payoff depending on the cross-sectional adoption share) is a distribution-dependent equilibrium the caller closes in the outer loop — exactly like Krusell–Smith's aggregate state, and just as much ✅ at the household-block level. (Class reference; exact canonical cite uncertain.)

> **§4 summary.** The household side of essentially every model is a faithful, no-bespoke-stage `∘`-chain over `MigrationStage`/`DirectedSearchStage`/`SectorSwitchingStage`/`LogitChoiceStage` (twelve built: `spatial`, `directed_search`, `sectoral`, `choo_siow`, `monte_redding_rrh`, `bryan_morten`, `tombe_zhu`, `diamond`, `menzio_shi`, `hsieh_hurst_jones_klenow`, `bayer_ferreira_mcmillan`, `technology_adoption`). The ◐s are of two kinds: a *genuine* primitive gap where Bayesian active-learning rides alongside the choice (Kennan–Walker, Arcidiacono — the subjective-backward ≠ objective-forward kernel, family C of the gaps), versus a merely *dimensional* per-sector-experience state-space blow-up that is composable in principle (Lee–Wolpin). The equilibrium objects some models then close — two-sided matching (Galichon–Salanié, two-sided Greenwood: the one-sided choice *is* a stage, the assignment fixed point is outer-loop), sorting prices/amenities, free-entry tightness, network externalities — are the caller's outer loop; see **Scope**. *The household chooses where to aim* is a stage; *the market that clears those aims against each other* is the outer loop.

### §5. Discrete jumps & optimal stopping; multi-axis composites; demographics

The workhorses here are `ArgmaxStage` (the `(max,+)` apply, brute or monotone, reward an `(after, before)` matrix, gating by `−Inf`), its sugars `BuyHomeStage`/`SellHomeStage`/`DefaultStage`, and the exit composite (`ExogenousExit`/`EndogenousExit`/`LogitEndogenousExit`). The structural insight for (i): **a discrete jump at a fixed cost, and optimal stopping, are the same object — an `ArgmaxStage` comparing "act (pay the fixed cost, jump)" against "do nothing."** The cost and post-jump consequences never bake into the choice; they compose as following `WealthChangeStage`/`MarkovStage` stages (the discipline `MigrationStage` uses for its move cost).

#### (i) Discrete jump at a fixed cost / optimal stopping
Non-convexity: a lump-sum cost paid only when acting makes the policy a band (inaction region + jump). The "value of acting net of the fixed cost vs. value of inaction" is a single `ArgmaxStage` (or its `LogitChoiceStage` smoothing); the non-convexity is a smoothness/adjoint matter (clean on the interior, kinked at the bands), not a missing primitive.

- **(S,s) durable adjustment (Caballero–Engel 1999)** — *mechanism:* adjust a lumpy durable only when the gap crosses a band. *Household block:* `ArgmaxStage(:durable)` over {keep, adjust} where the adjust value is an inner `ContinuousArgmaxStage` (= `DurableAdjustmentStage`) net of the fixed cost `∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅ (the nested keep/adjust→continuous-argmax composition of `examples/durable_housing`).
- **Durable (S,s) over the cycle (Berger–Vavra 2015)** — *mechanism:* responsiveness varies because the share near the band moves; Caballero–Engel mechanics with income risk. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ DurableAdjustmentStage ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅.
- **Home-equity extraction / refinancing (Bhutta–Keys 2016)** — *mechanism:* discrete equity-extraction episodes paying a refinance cost. *Household block:* a gated `ArgmaxStage` ({keep, refi/extract}) `∘ WealthChangeStage(refi cost, extracted cash) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ◐ (primitives suffice; no folder).
- **Durable (S,s) with transaction cost (Attanasio 2000)** — *mechanism:* infrequent lumpy car/durable purchases. *Household block:* `DurableAdjustmentStage ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅.
- **Housing with transaction cost (Díaz–Luengo-Prado 2008)** — *mechanism:* a proportional transaction cost makes owners adjust infrequently. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:h) ∘ MarkovStage(:income) ∘ BuyHomeStage ∘ IncomeStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(transaction fee) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Fidelity:* elegant — the cost rides the post-`BuyHomeStage` `WealthChangeStage`, never the choice. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/durable_housing`.
- **MPC out of housing wealth (Berger–Guerrieri–Lorenzoni–Vavra 2018)** — *mechanism:* house-price changes move consumption through a sufficient-statistic channel; owner/renter tenure choice under price risk. *Household block:* `AssetPriceChangeStage(revalue) ∘ BuyHomeStage/SellHomeStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(fee) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅ (all stages exist; see the `SellHomeStage` caveat below).
- **Housing boom–bust (Kaplan–Mitman–Violante 2020)** — *mechanism:* a two-asset (liquid+housing) economy where price beliefs drive a boom-bust; discrete own/rent with a transaction cost. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ BuyHomeStage/SellHomeStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(revaluation + cost) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:liquid)`. *Status:* ✅/◐ (tenure jump ✅; a mortgage linking housing and liquid invokes §5(ii); the boom-bust GE is an outer-loop price fixed point).
- **Sovereign default as optimal stopping (Eaton–Gersovitz 1981)** — *mechanism:* repay vs default (autarky/exclusion with an output penalty). *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ DefaultStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(debt reset) ∘ IncomeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MarkovStage(:status)`. *Fidelity:* `DefaultStage` is exactly the gated repay/default argmax. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/default`.
- **Sovereign default with spreads (Arellano 2008)** — *mechanism:* a defaultable bond priced by lenders, so default interacts with `q(a',y)`. *Household block:* `IncomeShock ∘ DefaultChoice ∘ DebtReset ∘ Receipt ∘ Savings ∘ Readmission` — the `examples/default` chain with the savings step priced, `q(a',y)` in the budget via `env`. *Build note:* `ConsumptionSavingsStage` expresses only a *unit*-price budget `c = before − after`, so it cannot apply a destination price `q(a',y)·a'`; the savings leg is instead a **raw `ArgmaxStage(:wealth)`** whose reward closure builds `M[a',x] = u(x − q(a',income)·a')` (a closure, no `src/` edit), with `a' ≥ 0` priced risk-free, the excluded branch forced to autarky, and the discount a separate `∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β)`. The `q`-schedule (an `(N_a, N_y)` matrix) is a damped fixed point in the driver — the schedule analogue of the Aiyagari scalar-`K` tatonnement. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/arellano_default` (household block) + outer-loop price fixed point.
- **Consumer bankruptcy (Chatterjee–Corbae–Nakajima–Ríos-Rull 2007)** — *mechanism:* a Chapter-7 filing discharges debt at the cost of an exclusion spell. *Household block:* `DefaultStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(discharge) ∘ MarkovStage(:status; exclusion) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅ (the consumer-bankruptcy reading of `examples/default`).
- **Consumer bankruptcy with fresh start (Livshits–MacGee–Tertilt 2007)** — *mechanism:* fresh-start vs garnishment regimes; life-cycle filers. *Household block:* `replicate_age(MarkovStage(:income) ∘ DefaultStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(discharge) ∘ WealthChangeStage(receipt; garnished `(1−λ)y` when excluded) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MarkovStage(:status; readmission ψ), N; axis=:age)` — the `examples/default` six-stage chain *verbatim*, wrapped in `replicate_age` and solved with the `life_cycle` finite-horizon driver under a no-negative-estate terminal array (a condition, not a stage). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/livshits_macgee_tertilt`.
- **Firm / establishment exit (Hopenhayn 1992)** — *mechanism:* an incumbent exits when continuation falls below scrap; free entry pins the entry margin. *Block:* `MarkovStage(:productivity) ∘ EndogenousExit(bequest = scrap) ∘ EntryStage`. *Fidelity:* exit is the built composite, entry the built source; free entry is an outer-loop scalar. *Status:* ◐ (stages tested in `test/test_exit.jl`/`test/test_entry_exit.jl`; no firm example folder).
- **Firm entry/exit dynamics (Clementi–Palazzo 2016)** — *mechanism:* entrants draw productivity, invest, exit endogenously. *Block:* `EntryStage(g) ∘ MarkovStage(:productivity) ∘ DurableAdjustmentStage(capital) ∘ EndogenousExit(scrap)`. *Status:* ◐ (primitives built; no folder).
- **Retirement timing / engine replacement (Rust 1987)** — *mechanism:* the canonical regenerative stopping — keep vs replace with EV-logit smoothing. *Block:* `MarkovStage(:mileage) ∘ LogitChoiceStage(:decision ∈ {keep, replace}) ∘ UtilityStage(−cost) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(:mileage; replace→0) ∘ ForgetfulSumStage(:decision)` — the regeneration is a *following* `WealthChangeStage` reset-to-0 reading the decision, the shock leading so the period boundary carries the transient decision axis at size 1 (Rust's expected-value `EV` form). *Fidelity:* Rust's logit-smoothed binary stopping is exactly `LogitChoiceStage` over a 2-level axis. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/rust_replacement` (shipped and verified solving; no bespoke stage — a clean `LogitChoiceStage` stopping block, no distinguishability wall).
- **Retirement with health & savings (French 2005)** — *mechanism:* joint retirement/consumption under health and mortality risk; retirement absorbing. *Block:* `replicate_age(MarkovStage(:health) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:work_status) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ ExogenousExit(survival, bequest), N)`. *Status:* ◐.
- **Social-security retirement / option value (Rust–Phelan 1997)** — *mechanism:* retirement timing responds to SS incentives via option value. *Block:* `replicate_age(ArgmaxStage/LogitChoiceStage(:work_status) ∘ WealthChangeStage(benefit accrual via env) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ AdvanceAgeStage(absorb_top=false))`. *Status:* ◐.
- **Technology / durable adoption & replacement (regenerative stopping)** — *mechanism:* adopt/replace when the output gain net of a fixed cost turns positive. *Block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:adopt_choice ∈ {keep, adopt}) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:vintage; adopt→top) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:wealth; receipt) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:wealth; pay F, adopters only) ∘ ForgetfulSumStage(:adopt_choice) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MarkovStage(:vintage; obsolescence)`. *Fidelity:* the adopt choice moves two axes (reset the vintage *and* charge `F` to fresh adopters only), so a plain gated `ArgmaxStage(:vintage)` cannot distinguish a fresh adopter from a standing top-vintage keeper sitting on the identical post-choice cell — it needs the **auxiliary-choice-axis pattern** (Route A, as in `habit`), the same distinguishability wall as `durable_housing`'s one-time-price note. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/vintage_replacement` (Route-A auxiliary axis; shipped and verified solving).
- **Mortgage refinancing / prepayment (LTV-gated; textbook / class example)** — *mechanism:* prepay/refinance when the rate gap less a cost and the LTV constraint make it worthwhile; the fixed cost makes adjustment lumpy (an (S,s) band in the balance). *Block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ MarkovStage(:hp) ∘ WealthChangeStage(receipt) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:refi_choice over balances) ∘ BorrowingConstraintStage(LTV m'/hp > θ → −Inf on cash-out) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:wealth; principal Δ + fixed cost κ + interest, reads old m and m') ∘ WealthChangeStage(:mortgage; set m') ∘ ForgetfulSumStage(:refi_choice) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Fidelity:* the simpler gated-`ArgmaxStage(:mortgage)`-then-`WealthChangeStage` pattern **cannot** express this — a following stage sees only the *post-choice* balance `m'`, so it can form neither the principal cash flow `(m'−m)` nor the one-time fixed cost `κ·1{m'≠m}` (both compare old and new); it needs the **Route-A auxiliary-choice axis** (as in `two_asset_hank`/`habit`/`durable_liquid`), the fixed cost riding the *following* WealthChange, never the choice reward. *Status:* ◐ 🏗 `examples/mortgage_refinancing` (Route-A auxiliary axis; shipped and verified solving; the LTV gate binds in the bust state).
- **Long-term defaultable mortgage / long-maturity debt (Campbell–Cocco 2015; Chatterjee–Eyigungor 2012)** — *mechanism:* a long-maturity debt balance amortizes deterministically each period while the borrower chooses, period by period, to keep paying, prepay, or default — gated by the loan-to-value ratio and a fixed cost. *Block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:debt; amortization drift) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:mortgage over {pay, prepay, default}, LTV `avail` → `−Inf`) ∘ WealthChangeStage(prepay cost / default discharge) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage` — an amortization-drift `WealthChangeStage` on the debt axis feeding the LTV-gated prepay/default `ArgmaxStage` (the §5(i) stopping shape). *Fidelity:* the amortization drift + LTV-gated discrete stopping is exactly §5(i); the long-maturity bond *pricing* (the lender's `q(b',y,d)` schedule for defaultable long-term debt) is the caller's outer loop. *Status:* ✅ (the borrower block) / ◐ (the gated multi-action stopping; long-maturity pricing outer-loop).

> **Caveat on `SellHomeStage` (verified in source).** `sell_home.jl` carries a `#TODO`: after the keep-vs-sell argmax, sellers and pre-existing renters both land on the renter slice, so crediting sale proceeds to *sellers only* needs the pre-sale size carried on another axis (or the fee folded into the buy stage's price). The keep/sell *choice* is exact; the seller-specific wealth credit is the one rough edge in the buy/sell pair. Models that net proceeds through `BuyHomeStage`'s price channel are unaffected.

#### (ii) Multi-axis composites (one choice moves two axes)
A single choice variable sets two state axes at once. This is **not** a gap. Two routes are built. **Route A (auxiliary-choice-axis):** route the choice onto a separate axis (the argmax populates it), so old state and new choice are both live; a downstream `WealthChangeStage` reads both; a final `ForgetfulSumStage` collapses the auxiliary axis. Cost: the intermediate tensor is `n_choice×` larger, and lumpy/non-monotone choices force the *brute* `ArgmaxStage`. **Route A′ (conserved-quantity reformulation):** when the coupling conserves a total (a transfer between two stocks), pick the conserved quantity as an axis and derive the other — the choice then moves only one axis. A dedicated **Route B** primitive ("one choice jointly sets two axes," lower memory) is **not built** — a recorded optimization, not a gap, since A/A′ already express these faithfully.

- **Rational addiction (Becker–Murphy 1988)** — *mechanism:* the chosen `c = x − b'` is consumed now and builds next period's addiction stock `S' = (1−δ_S)S + c`, with adjacent complementarity — one choice moves both wealth and the stock. *Household block (Route A):* `… ∘ ArgmaxStage(:savings_choice) ∘ UtilityStage(u(c,S)) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:habit, S') ∘ WealthChangeStage(:wealth, x↦b') ∘ ForgetfulSumStage(:savings_choice)`. *Fidelity:* intricate but exact — reinforcement breaks supermodularity, so the *brute* `ArgmaxStage` (not the monotone walk); pinned to a brute-Bellman reference. Route A′ does not apply (no conserved total). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/habit`.
- **Internal habit formation (Constantinides 1990)** — *mechanism:* CRRA felicity over the consumption *surplus* `c − γS`, the internal habit `S` a geometric average of past own consumption. *Household block (Route A):* byte-for-byte the `examples/habit` auxiliary-choice-axis chain — `Choose(:savings_choice) ∘ UtilityStage(u(c − γS)) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(:habit; S'=(1−δ)S+c) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:wealth; set b') ∘ ForgetfulSumStage` — with only the felicity changed. *Build note:* strict `−∞` subsistence cannot pass the `:brute` `ArgmaxStage` (which asserts every origin cell has one finite-reward action, and the minimum-wealth cell structurally has `c=0`); the standard quantitative device is to floor the surplus at a small `c_floor>0`, after which the floored region carries mass ≈2.5×10⁻⁵ (effectively unreachable), so the solve is numerically the subsistence model. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/constantinides_habit`.
- **Two-asset HANK (Kaplan–Moll–Violante 2018)** — *mechanism:* liquid + higher-return illiquid; a deposit/withdrawal pays a convex cost, so the illiquid choice jointly sets the illiquid stock and debits liquid — the wealthy-HtM mechanism. *Household block (Route A):* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:liquid, receipt) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:illiquid_choice) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:liquid, debit reads both choice and old illiquid) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:illiquid) ∘ ForgetfulSumStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:liquid)`. *Fidelity:* intricate (the `n_a×` auxiliary axis) but exact — verified vs brute. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/two_asset_hank`.
- **Two-asset HANK, portfolio-value form (Route A′)** — *mechanism:* identical economics; exploit that a deposit conserves `W = b + a`. Take `W` and `a` as axes; `b = W − a` is derived, so the illiquid choice moves only the illiquid axis. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:wealth, ReturnsW) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:illiquid, ReturnsA) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:illiquid, reward = −χ(a'−a)) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:wealth, consume) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage(β)`. *Fidelity:* elegant — single-axis choices, no auxiliary axis, no `n_choice×` blowup; **bit-exact (max diff 0.0)** vs the brute `(W,a)` Bellman. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/two_asset_hank_pv` (the strictly cleaner route).
- **Two-asset HANK / sequence-space Jacobian (Auclert–Bardóczy–Rognlie–Straub 2021)** — the same household block; the SSJ transition is outer-loop (cf. `examples/aiyagari_mit_shock`). *Status:* ✅ (household block) + outer-loop SSJ.
- **Durable + liquid asset (Berger–Vavra 2015; Díaz–Luengo-Prado 2010)** — *mechanism:* a lumpy durable financed alongside a liquid asset; the durable choice sets the stock *and* debits liquid by the down-payment + fixed cost (two axes at once). *Household block (Route A):* `MarkovStage(:durable; depreciation) ∘ MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:liquid; receipt) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:durable_choice; :brute) ∘ WealthChangeStage(:liquid; debit p·(d'−d)+F·1{d'≠d}, reads old d and d') ∘ WealthChangeStage(:durable; set d') ∘ ForgetfulSumStage(:durable_choice) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:liquid)`. *Build note:* the depreciation `MarkovStage` keeps the (S,s) margin live; an `ε`-floor on post-transaction liquid (the two-asset-HANK device) keeps every action feasible for the `:brute` argmax, the consumption hit deterring unaffordable adjustments; the adjustment rate `∫1{d'≠d}dΛ` is a *transition* statistic recovered driver-side. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/durable_liquid` (Route-A auxiliary axis).
- **Portfolio + consumption, jointly (Cocco–Gomes–Maenhout 2005)** — *mechanism:* jointly choose consumption (a level) and the risky share (a dispersion) — two *features*, not two grid axes. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ MeanVarianceStage` (no auxiliary axis). *Fidelity:* elegant — the share chooses a *kernel feature*, so neither Route A nor A′ is needed; the two choices simply compose. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/portfolio`.

#### (iii) Demographics — entry/exit/aging
Demographics split along the forward/backward seam. **Exit/death** needs no new machinery: `MarkovStage` accepts a sub-stochastic transition, so the forward loses dying mass and the backward `Kᵀ` gives a survival-weighted continuation; `AdvanceAgeStage(absorb_top=false)` is the OLG instance. **Entry/birth** is the one piece no linear kernel synthesizes (`Λ' = K·Λ + g`) — closed by `EntryStage` (forward `Λ += g`, backward identity). The symmetric value-of-death sink is the exit composite, with `bequest` *required* (with CRRA σ>1 a default bequest of 0 would make the poor prefer death).

- **OLG / life-cycle (Auerbach–Kotlikoff 1987)** — *mechanism:* finite-lived cohorts save for retirement and bequests. *Household block:* `replicate_age(MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(age-dependent earnings) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage, N; axis=:age)`, `AdvanceAgeStage` rolling cohorts, newborn inflow from the driver. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/life_cycle`.
- **OLG with social security (Conesa–Krueger 1999)** — *mechanism:* finite lives + idiosyncratic risk + an unfunded PAYG SS system. *Household block:* `replicate_age(MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(b ↦ (1+r)b + (1−τ)y(age)ε + benefit) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage, N; axis=:age)` — the `life_cycle` chain with the SS tax/benefit riding the receipt closure (age and SS rules from `env`), no bespoke stage. *Build note:* the driver runs the finite-horizon backward sweep + forward cohort simulation, plus a PAYG balance loop pinning the benefit `b·(#retired) = τ·(aggregate earnings)` (a one-shot fixed point under inelastic labor). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/conesa_krueger`.
- **Perpetual youth (Blanchard 1985; Yaari 1965)** — *mechanism:* a constant death hazard `δ` + actuarially-fair annuities → a stationary population. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(annuity receipt (1+r)/(1−δ)·b + wy) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ ExogenousExit(survival 1−δ, bequest) ∘ EntryStage(g; Σg=δ)`. *Fidelity:* the survival discount `β·s` in the Euler equation cancels the annuity gross-up `1/s` to the standard `β(1+r)` Aiyagari condition; the forward mass map `M_{t+1}=(1−δ)M_t+Σg` fixes the population at `M*=1`. With death exogenous the bequest level shifts `V` but not the policy or `Λ`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/perpetual_youth`.
- **Perpetual youth with a life-cycle profile (Gertler 1999)** — *mechanism:* a worker/retiree two-stage structure on top of the death hazard. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:life_stage) ∘ ExogenousExit(stage-dependent survival, bequest) ∘ EntryStage(newborn workers) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage`. *Status:* ✅.
- **Bequests & wealth concentration (De Nardi 2004)** — *mechanism:* voluntary + accidental bequests with joy-of-giving fatten the top tail. *Household block:* `replicate_age(MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(receipt) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth) ∘ ExogenousExit(survival, bequest), N; axis=:age)` — exit applies at *every* age (accidental bequests throughout, not only terminal), the warm-glow `bequest = φ·u(a'+κ)` exactly the *required* `bequest` field (`κ` makes bequests a luxury — De Nardi's fat-tail device). *Fidelity:* elegant — the dynastic link is `EntryStage`'s `g`, closed by a dynastic fixed point on the newborn inherited-wealth distribution (deaths telescope to one newborn per death). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/de_nardi_bequests`.
- **Medical expenses & mortality in old age (De Nardi–French–Jones 2010)** — *mechanism:* the elderly save against uncertain medical expenses and mortality; high near-death expense risk explains slow dissaving. *Household block:* `replicate_age(MarkovStage(:health) ∘ WealthChangeStage(x = max((1+r)w + pension − m(health,age), c_floor)) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:wealth) ∘ ExogenousExit(survival = s(health, age), bequest), N; axis=:age)` — the stochastic medical expense reads the (random) health state and age, `c_floor` the means-tested (Medicaid) floor; health-and-age mortality is `ExogenousExit`'s `survival` dep closure, the cohort thinning with no entry. *Fidelity:* the survivor wealth profile dissaves slowly (precautionary buffer + bequest). *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/de_nardi_french_jones`.
- **Entry / birth & population growth (`EntryStage`)** — *mechanism:* new agents born/enter at an exogenous or targeted rate; birth ≠ death moves the population. *Household block:* `MarkovStage(:income) ∘ WealthChangeStage(receipt) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage ∘ ExogenousExit(survival s) ∘ EntryStage(entry = g)` — `g` any `ScalarField`; the forward total mass collapses to the affine recursion `M_{t+1} = s·M_t + Σg`, fixed point `M* = Σg/(1−s)` (replacement `Σg=1−s ⇒ M*=1`; birth-heavy `Σg=2(1−s) ⇒ M*=2`). *Build note:* because `EntryStage` is a *fixed additive* source the map is affine, not geometric — genuine exponential growth would need a mass-proportional source, a deliberate modelling choice of `EntryStage`, not a limitation worked around. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/entry_birth`.

- **Endogenous fertility / quantity–quality (Barro–Becker 1989; Jones–Schoonbroodt 2010; Greenwood–Seshadri–Vandenbroucke 2005; de la Croix–Doepke 2003)** — *mechanism:* parents choose the *number* of children — a chosen birth rate — trading child quantity against per-child quality (human-capital investment). *Household block:* the chosen-`g` inflow is `EntryStage` with `g` a closure reading the fertility policy (`entry = (; dep…, env) -> birth_rate · g_shape`), the birth rate itself an `ArgmaxStage` over a `:fertility` axis (or a `ReproductionStage` whose growth factor `s` is driven by the choice); the quantity–quality tradeoff couples that fertility choice to a child-human-capital `CapitalInvestmentStage(:h_child)` (cross-ref §1 child investment — Becker–Tomes, Daruich), closed across cohorts by `ReproductionStage`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — the chosen-`g` source is exactly `EntryStage` (its `g` is any `ScalarField`, including a chosen-rate closure) and the per-child investment is clean `CapitalInvestmentStage`, but the cross-generation quantity–quality coupling (fertility ↔ per-child `h` ↔ the next cohort's mass *and* state) is an intricate cross-generation composition + cohort-iterating driver, like the Becker–Tomes ◐s in §1. *Status:* ◐.

> **§5 summary.** The discrete-jump/stopping models are uniformly `ArgmaxStage`/`LogitChoiceStage`-act-vs-wait with the cost and consequences composed downstream — now shipped across `default`, `durable_housing`, sovereign default with spreads (`arellano_default`), life-cycle bankruptcy (`livshits_macgee_tertilt`), engine replacement (`rust_replacement`), vintage adoption (`vintage_replacement`), and LTV-gated mortgage refinancing (`mortgage_refinancing`). The composites are all expressible via Route A (`habit`, `constantinides_habit`, `two_asset_hank`, `durable_liquid`, and the Route-A `vintage_replacement`/`mortgage_refinancing` stopping builds) or the cleaner Route A′ (`two_asset_hank_pv`); a dedicated Route-B primitive is an open optimization, not a gap. Demographics are closed by `EntryStage` + the exit composite + `replicate_age`/`AdvanceAgeStage` — shipped in `entry_birth`, `perpetual_youth`, `conesa_krueger`, `de_nardi_bequests`, and `de_nardi_french_jones`. The recurring dogfooding lesson: a fixed/one-time cost that must distinguish an actor from a non-actor sitting on the identical post-choice cell needs the Route-A auxiliary-choice axis (the `durable_housing` distinguishability wall), reused verbatim across `durable_liquid`/`mortgage_refinancing`/`vintage_replacement`; no new stage surfaced. The outer-loop objects some models then need (two-sided matching, distribution-as-state, the Arellano `q`-schedule) are the caller's outer loop; the genuine household-stage gap (G3) is consolidated below.

### §6. Firm dynamics — the same stages, reinterpreted

This package builds *agent blocks*: a within-period chain of stages mapping a continuation value `V` (backward) and a distribution `Λ` (forward), glued by `∘` (time-ordered) and `⊕` (direct sum over an axis). Nothing in that machinery knows whether the agent is a household or a firm. A firm's recursion — produce, draw a shock, invest, set a price, hire, possibly exit — is the *same object* as a household's under a dictionary that relabels the axes, the reward closures, and the stages. The catalog's scope, "household blocks only," means *agent blocks with no outer loop*: the package supplies the within-period `V`/`Λ` chain and leaves the equilibrium to the caller. For a firm that division of labor is exactly the same as for Aiyagari — the **free-entry condition, the price level, the wage, market clearing, and the firm-size distribution as an aggregate state are the caller's outer loop**, never a deficiency of the block. So every firm-dynamics model whose firm problem *is* an agent recursion is in scope precisely as a household block is.

#### The dictionary

| Household reading | Firm reading | Stage |
|---|---|---|
| wealth `b` | capital `k` / net worth | the operative continuous axis |
| income / employment shock | productivity / demand shock | `MarkovStage` |
| saving / human-capital investment | physical / R&D / intangible investment | `ContinuousArgmaxStage`, `DurableAdjustmentStage`/`CapitalInvestmentStage` |
| convex saving / effort cost | convex capital-adjustment cost | the `adjustment_cost` closure in `DurableAdjustmentStage` |
| (S,s) durable purchase | lumpy / fixed-cost investment | keep/adjust `ArgmaxStage` (§5(i)) |
| consumption / payout | dividend / payout | `ConsumptionSavingsStage` |
| bequest / value of death | scrap / liquidation value | the **required** `bequest` field of the exit composite |
| death | firm exit | `ExogenousExit`/`EndogenousExit`/`LogitEndogenousExit` |
| birth | firm entry | `EntryStage` (the additive `Λ += g` source) |
| portfolio / risky share | leverage / financial structure | `MeanVarianceStage`/`PortfolioStage` |
| borrowing constraint | collateral / financing constraint | `BorrowingConstraintStage` |
| consumer default | firm / debt default | `DefaultStage` |
| (S,s) durable adjustment | menu-cost price-setting | keep/adjust `ArgmaxStage` over the price axis (§5(i)) |
| rational inattention | inattentive / sticky-information pricing | `ScaleVarianceStage`/`LogitChoiceStage` (entropy member) |
| job search effort | vacancy posting / hiring | `SearchMatchingStage` |

Firm models are organized by the *same control-mechanism families* as the household catalog; the only difference is the label on the axis. **✗** is a genuine missing *agent* primitive — never an outer-loop object.

#### Capital investment — the `DurableAdjustmentStage` family

The firm's `k'`-choice is the household's stock-investment choice with `capital ↔ stock`. The **convex** case is `DurableAdjustmentStage(:k)` verbatim (the `service` closure carries the profit function `π(k';env)`); the **lumpy** case is the §5(i) keep/adjust `ArgmaxStage` whose adjust value is an inner continuous argmax net of a fixed cost — the (S,s) shape shipped in `examples/durable_housing`.

- **Convex capital adjustment / q-theory (Cooper–Haltiwanger 2006; Hayashi 1982)** — *firm mechanism:* choose `k'` paying a smooth convex cost `φ(k'−k)`; marginal `q` = marginal adjustment cost. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:z) ∘ DurableAdjustmentStage(:k; service = π(k';env), adjustment_cost = φ) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:payout)`. *Fidelity:* exemplary — exactly what `DurableAdjustmentStage` is for. *Status:* ✅ (covered by `examples/capital_investment` — the §1 household twin, `wealth↔capital`; not duplicated as a firm folder).
- **Lumpy / non-convex investment (Khan–Thomas 2008; Bachmann–Caballero–Engel 2013; Cooper–Haltiwanger 2006 fixed-cost component)** — *firm mechanism:* a fixed cost makes investment lumpy and bursty; an inaction band. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:z) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:k over {keep, adjust}; reward M[k',k] = −F·1{k'≠k}, :brute)` — the (S,s) keep/adjust idiom, the adjust value an inner continuous argmax net of the fixed cost. *Fidelity:* the §5(i) keep/adjust nesting, `durable→capital`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/lumpy_investment` (block; the cross-sectional capital distribution + prices are outer loop).
- **Generalized (S,s) investment (Caballero–Engel 1999)** — *firm mechanism:* a *distribution* of adjustment hazards rather than a sharp band. *Agent block:* the keep/adjust choice smoothed to `LogitChoiceStage(:k over {keep, adjust}; ε)`. *Fidelity:* the adjustment-hazard function is the logit probability. *Status:* ✅ (covered-by-pattern in `examples/lumpy_investment`'s README — the `LogitChoiceStage(ε)` swap).

#### Firm entry & exit — the exit composite + `EntryStage`

`firm exit ↔ death`, `firm entry ↔ birth`, `scrap / liquidation value ↔ bequest` (the required field of the exit composite). Free entry is a scalar outer-loop fixed point, never scored against the block.

- **Hopenhayn (1992)** — *firm mechanism:* an incumbent exits when continuation falls below scrap; free entry pins the cutoff. *Agent block:* `EntryStage(g = entrant draws) ∘ UtilityStage(π) ∘ EndogenousExit(bequest = scrap) ∘ TimeDiscountingStage ∘ MarkovStage(:z)` — exit the built `EndogenousExit` (`max(V, scrap)`), entry the built source, free entry an outer-loop scalar. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/hopenhayn`.
- **Hopenhayn–Rogerson (1993)** — *firm mechanism:* Hopenhayn + a firing cost, for employment-protection policy. *Agent block:* the Hopenhayn chain with an `ArgmaxStage(:n over {keep, adjust})` leg carrying the firing cost. *Status:* ◐ (covered-by-pattern in `examples/hopenhayn`'s README; not separately built).
- **Clementi–Palazzo (2016)** — *firm mechanism:* entrants draw productivity, invest, exit endogenously, amplifying fluctuations. *Agent block:* `EntryStage(g) ∘ MarkovStage(:z) ∘ DurableAdjustmentStage(:k) ∘ EndogenousExit(bequest = scrap)`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/clementi_palazzo`.
- **Cooley–Quadrini (2001)** — *firm mechanism:* entry/exit + financial frictions + default, so size/age interact with leverage. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:z) ∘ DurableAdjustmentStage(:k) ∘ DefaultStage(:debt) ∘ MeanVarianceStage(leverage) ∘ EndogenousExit(scrap) ∘ EntryStage(g)`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — every leg is built, but capital and debt/financing couple *two* axes, so a faithful block needs the **Route-A auxiliary-axis composite** (cf. `examples/two_asset_hank`), not a one-axis straight `∘`-chain — buildable-by-pattern, the firm twin of the §1 child-investment ◐s, not a missing primitive. *Status:* ◐ (Route-A buildable-by-pattern; not separately built).

#### Financial frictions & financing — `BorrowingConstraintStage` / `DefaultStage`

`collateral constraint ↔ borrowing constraint`, `leverage ↔ portfolio`, `firm default ↔ consumer default`.

- **Collateral-constrained investment (Buera–Shin 2013; Midrigan–Xu 2014; Moll 2014)** — *firm mechanism:* operate capital only up to a multiple of net worth, so misallocation and slow self-financing persist. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:z) ∘ BorrowingConstraintStage(infeasible = (…;env) -> k > λ·a) ∘ DurableAdjustmentStage(:k) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:net_worth)`. *Fidelity:* faithful — the constraint is the `−Inf` special case. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/collateral_investment` (block; `r`/wage + wealth distribution outer loop).
- **Defaultable firm debt (Gomes 2001; Cooley–Quadrini 2001; Arellano–Bai–Zhang 2012)** — *firm mechanism:* a levered firm repays or defaults; lenders price risk into `q(b',z)`. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:z) ∘ DefaultStage ∘ WealthChangeStage(:debt; q(b',z) from env) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:payout)`. *Fidelity:* the firm reading of `examples/default`. *Status:* ✅ (block) + outer-loop debt-pricing.
- **Banking / financial intermediaries (Gertler–Kiyotaki 2010; Gertler–Karadi 2011)** — *firm mechanism:* a banker levers net worth into risky assets up to an incentive (moral-hazard) constraint tying leverage to franchise value, pays out and exits at a constant survival rate, and is replaced by new bankers seeded with start-up funds. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:return) ∘ AssetPriceChangeStage(net-worth revaluation) ∘ MeanVarianceStage(leverage) ∘ BorrowingConstraintStage(incentive/leverage constraint via env) ∘ ConsumptionSavingsStage(:payout) ∘ ExogenousExit(banker survival, bequest = retained funds) ∘ EntryStage(new bankers)`. *Fidelity:* every leg is built — leverage `MeanVarianceStage`, the incentive `−Inf` gate, payout, the exit composite, new-banker `EntryStage` — but net worth and leverage couple *two* axes, so a faithful block needs the **Route-A auxiliary-axis composite** (cf. `examples/two_asset_hank`/Cooley–Quadrini), buildable-by-pattern rather than separately built, and the deposit rate / intermediated-asset price clear in the outer loop. *Status:* ◐ (Route-A buildable-by-pattern; the asset price + spread are the caller's outer loop).

#### Price-setting — menu costs and inattention

A relative price is a *stock* eroding with inflation, reset at a cost. `menu-cost pricing ↔ (S,s) durable`.

- **Fixed menu cost (Golosov–Lucas 2007; Nakamura–Steinsson 2010)** — *firm mechanism:* reset the nominal price only when the inflation-eroded, shock-buffeted gap warrants the menu cost. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:cost_shock) ∘ MarkovStage(:price; inflation erosion) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:price over {keep, reset})` with the reset value an inner continuous argmax net of the menu cost. *Fidelity:* §5(i) keep/adjust with `durable→price`, `transaction cost→menu cost`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/menu_cost` (block; the monetary block is outer loop).
- **Generalized state-dependent pricing (Dotsey–King–Wolman 1999)** — *firm mechanism:* a *distribution* of menu costs → a time-varying fraction of adjusters. *Agent block:* the reset choice as `LogitChoiceStage(:price over {keep, reset}; ε)`. *Status:* ✅ (covered-by-pattern in `examples/menu_cost`'s README — swap the keep/reset `ArgmaxStage` for `LogitChoiceStage(ε)`).
- **(S,s) pricing & the Phillips curve (Gertler–Leahy 2008)** — *agent block:* the menu-cost keep/reset chain; the Phillips-curve aggregation is outer loop. *Status:* ✅ (block).
- **Inattentive producer (Reis 2006)** — *firm mechanism:* pay a fixed cost to *update information* infrequently, acting on a stale estimate between updates. *Agent block:* an `ArgmaxStage` over {update, stay-inattentive} on an information-vintage axis carrying the outdated estimate `∘ MarkovStage(:z)`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — the update *choice* is clean §5(i) stopping, but acting on a stale estimate between updates needs an information-vintage/belief axis no stage populates — the **family C** active-learning / information-vintage-kernel gap (gaps item 7, the subjective-backward ≠ objective-forward kernel; same root as Kennan–Walker), not a new firm-specific gap. *Status:* ◐ (the stale-info-vintage piece points to the family C gap).
- **Sticky information (Mankiw–Reis 2002)** — *firm mechanism:* information diffuses at a Calvo hazard; a fraction reprice on outdated info. *Agent block:* `MarkovStage(:information_age; Calvo hazard)` over a vintage axis. *Status:* ◐ (the information-vintage axis is the family C gap, gaps item 7 — same as Reis 2006).
- **Rationally inattentive pricing (Maćkowiak–Wiederholt 2009)** — *firm mechanism:* allocate finite Shannon attention across aggregate vs idiosyncratic conditions. *Agent block:* `ScaleVarianceStage(:price_signal; cost = λ·info(θ)) ∘` the aggregate `MarkovStage` — the firm twin of household RI (`examples/rational_inattention`, §2). *Fidelity:* single-signal ✅; the multi-signal attention budget couples several precisions through one Shannon constraint — the **family B** G3 gap (gaps item 5), identical to the household, not a firm-specific one. *Status:* ◐/✗ (single-signal ✅; the coupled multi-signal budget is the family B gap).

#### Labor demand & hiring — `ArgmaxStage` (S,s) + `SearchMatchingStage`

`(S,s) hiring/firing ↔ (S,s) durable`, `vacancy posting ↔ search effort`.

- **Lumpy employment adjustment (Cooper–Haltiwanger–Willis 2007; Caballero–Engel–Haltiwanger 1997)** — *agent block:* `MarkovStage(:z) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:n over {keep, adjust}; reward −F·1{n'≠n})` with the adjust value `n'` net of the fixed cost. *Fidelity:* §5(i) on the employment axis, `capital→labor`. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/lumpy_labor`.
- **Vacancy posting / hiring (firm side of Mortensen–Pissarides 1994)** — *agent block:* `SearchMatchingStage(:employment; effort = vacancies, hazard = q(θ) via FromEnv) ∘ DurableAdjustmentStage(:k)`. *Status:* ✅ (the firm side of `examples/search_matching` — vacancy posting is the search-effort twin; tightness `θ` outer loop).

#### Inventories & innovation

- **Inventory (S,s) (Khan–Thomas 2007; Kahn–McConnell–Pérez-Quirós 2002)** — *agent block:* `MarkovStage(:demand) ∘ ArgmaxStage(:inventory over {keep, reorder}; reward −F·1{s'≠s})` with the reorder value the target stock net of the order cost. *Status:* ✅ 🏗 `examples/inventory_ss` (block).
- **Quality ladders / firm-as-portfolio (Klette–Kortum 2004)** — *firm mechanism:* convex R&D adds product lines (innovation arrivals); creative destruction removes them; size = line count. *Agent block:* `DurableAdjustmentStage(:knowledge; adjustment_cost = R&D) ∘ MarkovStage(:n_lines; birth at chosen R&D, death at the aggregate destruction hazard via FromEnv)`. *Fidelity:* ◐ — convex R&D is clean `DurableAdjustmentStage`, but the line-*count* birth–death is a counting process whose birth rate is the firm's *own* R&D choice (a within-block choice wired into a `MarkovStage` transition) → **Route-A territory** (cf. `examples/two_asset_hank`/Cooley–Quadrini), buildable-by-pattern, not a missing primitive. *Status:* ◐ (Route-A buildable-by-pattern; not separately built).
- **Heterogeneous innovation (Akcigit–Kerr 2018)** — *agent block:* internal/external R&D legs `∘` the line-count birth-death `MarkovStage`. *Status:* ◐ (same Route-A line-count structure as Klette–Kortum).

> **§6 summary.** Firm dynamics is the household catalog read through one dictionary. Seven firm folders now ship and verify solving: Hopenhayn entry/exit (`hopenhayn`), Clementi–Palazzo entry+invest+exit (`clementi_palazzo`), lumpy investment (`lumpy_investment`), lumpy labor (`lumpy_labor`), menu-cost pricing (`menu_cost`), (S,s) inventory (`inventory_ss`), and collateral-constrained investment (`collateral_investment`) — every one a `∘`/`⊕` of existing stages, no bespoke stage, no `src/` bug surfaced. The (S,s) keep/adjust idiom is a one-liner — `M[x',x] = −F·1{x'≠x}` fed to a `:brute` `ArgmaxStage` — reused verbatim across capital, labor, price, and inventory. Covered, not duplicated: convex capital adjustment (`capital_investment`), firm/debt default (`default`), vacancy posting (`search_matching`); covered-by-pattern: Hopenhayn–Rogerson firing cost and Dotsey–King–Wolman / generalized-(S,s) (a `LogitChoiceStage(ε)` swap), each noted in a folder README. The remaining ◐s are *buildable-by-pattern*, not missing primitives: Cooley–Quadrini and Gertler–Kiyotaki/Karadi couple two axes (capital + financing) and need the Route-A auxiliary-axis composite (cf. `two_asset_hank`), and Klette–Kortum/Akcigit–Kerr quality ladders pair clean convex R&D with a line-count counting process whose birth rate is the firm's own choice (again Route-A territory). The genuine gaps are *shared* with the household, not firm-specific: the inattentive-producer / sticky-information models (Reis 2006; Mankiw–Reis 2002) point to the family C information-vintage / subjective-backward-kernel gap, and coupled multi-signal RI pricing (Maćkowiak–Wiederholt) is the family B G3 gap. Everything a firm needs beyond its block — free entry, the price level, the wage, clearing, the firm-size distribution — is the caller's outer loop, exactly as for Aiyagari.

### §7. Sign-flips — the "negative" of each stage

Many stages carry a **signed control**, and the *opposite sign* is a distinct, separately-named economic model computed by the *very same code*. Concavity becomes convexity; a maximization becomes a minimization; a source becomes a sink; a discount factor below one becomes a growth factor above one. Each flip costs nothing — no new kernel, often not even a branch (the `LogitChoiceStage` log-sum-exp is sign-agnostic; see `logit_choice.jl`). So the framework earns a **second family of models for free**, one per axis below. The headline case is the entropy member: `ε > 0` is discrete choice, `ε < 0` is Hansen–Sargent robustness — the same `ε·log Σ exp(·/ε)` read in opposite curvature.

#### The "Opposites" table

| Stage (signed control) | `+` side | `−` side | Firm-dynamics dual (§6) |
|---|---|---|---|
| `LogitChoiceStage` scale `ε` | **logit / discrete-RI choice** (`ε>0`, soft-MAX) | **robustness / multiplier prefs** (`ε<0`, soft-MIN) | ambiguity-averse / robust firm |
| `MeanVarianceStage` exposure `θ` (curvature of `V`) | **insure / hedge** (concave `V`, `θ↓`) | **gamble / risk-shift** (convex `V`, `θ↑`) | hedging vs risk-taking leverage |
| `ScaleVarianceStage` dispersion `θ` | **rational inattention** — pay to *sharpen* (`θ↓`) | **costly diffusion** — pay to *add* noise (`θ↑`) | firm acquiring info vs experimenting / probing demand |
| `RetentionStage` vs `SearchMatchingStage` (hazard sign) | **search** — pay TO transition | **retain** — pay NOT to transition (`K_A=I`) | post a vacancy / acquire vs retain a worker / customer |
| `EntryStage` vs exit composite (mass sign) | **entry** — source (`Λ += g`) | **exit** — sink | firm entry vs firm exit |
| `PointwiseScaleStage` / `TimeDiscountingStage` factor | **growth / reproduction** (`β>1`) | **discounting / impatience** (`β<1`) | expanding firm vs discounting firm |
| `WealthChangeStage` / `DeterministicContinuousStage` drift | **accumulation** (drift up) | **depreciation / decay** (drift down) | capital accumulation vs depreciation |
| `ArgmaxStage` extremum | **maximize reward** (value) | **minimize cost** (negate reward) | value-maximizing agent vs cost-minimizing firm/planner |

#### The flips

- **`LogitChoiceStage` scale `ε` — choice ↔ robustness (the headline negative).** The backward writes `V_start = ε·log Σ_j exp((−C + V_end)/ε)`. `ε > 0` = the **soft-MAX**: logit/Gumbel discrete choice (McFadden 1974), discrete RI at small `ε` (Matějka–McKay 2015). `ε < 0` = the **soft-MIN**: the min-over-distortions entropic certainty equivalent — **robustness / multiplier preferences** (Hansen–Sargent 2008; Hansen–Sargent–Tallarini 1999), multiplier `|ε|`. The stability shift is sign-correct either way — *no branch*; robustness is literally the logit stage at negative scale. Firm dual: an ambiguity-averse firm. *Status:* ✅ / ✅ (both built; §2, §4).
- **`MeanVarianceStage` exposure `θ` — insure ↔ gamble.** Concave `V` ⇒ `θ↓` = **insure / hedge** (Yaari 1965; Merton 1969). Convex `V` ⇒ `θ↑` = **gamble / risk-shift** (Vereshchagina–Hopenhayn 2009). One stage, both sides of `V`'s curvature, no skewness primitive. Firm dual: hedging vs risk-taking leverage. The gamble side is a built example (`examples/risk_shifting`). *Status:* ✅ / ✅.
- **`ScaleVarianceStage` dispersion `θ` — sharpen ↔ diffuse.** `θ↓` = **rational inattention**, pay to *reduce* posterior variance (Sims 2003; Luo 2008). `θ↑` = **costly diffusion**, pay to *add* dispersion: experimentation/exploration (Bolton–Harris 1999; Weitzman 1979 Pandora's box), deliberate randomization, information design (Kamenica–Gentzkow 2011). Firm dual: a firm acquiring info vs probing demand. *Status:* ✅ (RI) / ✅ 🏗 `examples/costly_diffusion` — the bare convex-`V`-driven diffusion is a pure `ScaleVarianceStage` composition (limited-liability floor convexifies `V`); the experimentation / information-design *models* that additionally want a tracked belief axis remain ◐ (family C).
- **`RetentionStage` vs `SearchMatchingStage` — the hazard sign.** Search = **pay TO transition** (find a job/match; Mortensen 1970). Retention (`K_A=I`) = **pay NOT to transition** (survive, stay employed, prevention; the conjugate blend `V = K_B·V + c*(K_A·V − K_B·V)`). Firm dual: recruit vs retain. Both built (`examples/insurance`, `examples/search_matching`). *Status:* ✅ / ✅.
- **`EntryStage` vs the exit composite — the mass sign.** `EntryStage` = **source** (births/firm entry, `Λ += g`; Hopenhayn entry, Blanchard 1985). Exit composite = **sink** (deaths/firm exit). Firm dual: firm entry vs exit. *Status:* ✅ / ✅ (cross-ref §5(iii), §6).
- **`PointwiseScaleStage` / `TimeDiscountingStage` factor — grow ↔ discount.** `β<1` = **discounting/impatience** (`TimeDiscountingStage`). `β>1` = **growth/reproduction** (`ReproductionStage`, up-weighting mass): population/colony growth, expanding firms. Firm dual: expanding vs discounting firm. *Status:* ✅ / ✅.
- **`WealthChangeStage` / `DeterministicContinuousStage` drift — accumulate ↔ depreciate.** Drift up = **accumulation** (returns, deposits, capital build-up). Drift down = **depreciation/decay/spend-down** (durable decay, habit decay, debt paydown). Firm dual: the two halves of `k' = (1−δ)k + i`. *Status:* ✅ / ✅.
- **`ArgmaxStage` extremum — maximize ↔ minimize.** Maximize reward = the value-maximizing agent (every §5(i) stopping problem). Minimize cost = negate the reward → a cost-minimizing planner/firm. A one-line sign on the reward, not a second stage. Firm dual: value-maximizing agent vs cost-minimizing firm. *Status:* ✅ / ✅.

> **§7 summary.** Eight signed axes, each a named model on both sides from one body of code — and after `examples/costly_diffusion`, **every sign-flip is realized by a shipped, verified example**. No flip is a ✗; the only residual ◐ is the experimentation / information-design *reading* of costly diffusion, which additionally wants a tracked belief axis (family C) — the bare convex-`V`-driven diffusion itself is now shipped.

### Genuine household-stage gaps

A *household-stage gap* is a household choice the current stage vocabulary cannot yet express — **not**
something a model needs from outside the household block. The out-of-scope objects (within-period
equilibria, firm/production blocks, market clearing, two-sided matching, the
distribution-as-aggregate-state recursion, planner/contract problems) are the **outer loop**, which
this package leaves to the caller for *every* model (see **Scope**); they are never gaps in a
household-stages package. With that line drawn, the genuine household-stage gaps are few — and the gaps
recorded historically (the kernel-choice family, entry/birth, risk-sensitivity, brute continuous argmax)
are all now **closed** by `MeanVarianceStage`/`ScaleVarianceStage`, `EntryStage`, `LogitChoiceStage` at
`ε<0`, and `ContinuousArgmaxStage`. The genuine gaps fall into three families. The first two are a missing **backward aggregator**, not a missing source, kernel, or choice axis: stages compose the continuation *affinely* (`V_start = reward + K·V_end`, scaled by an affine `TimeDiscountingStage`), the only non-linear backward map in the vocabulary being `LogitChoiceStage`'s log-sum-exp, and these gaps are exactly the places that need a *different* non-affine map. The third is of a distinct kind — a stage whose **backward and forward kernels differ**, which the single-kernel stage abstraction cannot represent.

#### A — non-affine value / time aggregation
1. **Epstein–Zin / Kreps–Porteus recursive utility (Epstein–Zin 1989; Kreps–Porteus 1978).** A CES time-aggregator wrapping a nonlinear (CRRA) certainty equivalent of the continuation, `V = [(1−β)u^ρ + β·(𝔼 V^α)^{ρ/α}]^{1/ρ}` — separating risk aversion from the EIS. Only the *entropic* special case (risk-sensitive control, Hansen–Sargent–Tallarini 1999) is reachable, via `LogitChoiceStage` at `ε<0`; the general EZ aggregator — the long-run-risk, EZ-HANK, and asset-pricing workhorse — is not. *Status:* ✗ missing primitive (a recursive-utility backward aggregator). **The single most central absence.**
2. **Quasi-hyperbolic / present-biased discounting (Laibson 1997; β–δ).** Time-inconsistency breaks a stage invariant: the policy optimizes `J = u + βδ·W'`, but the value *propagated* to earlier selves is `W = u + δ·W'` — choice and continuation-welfare use *different* discounting. Needs a "choose under one continuation, propagate a second" (Markov-perfect self-control) apply. *Status:* ✗ missing primitive — ◐ if built as a bespoke outer-loop alternation. (Time-*consistent* Gul–Pesendorfer temptation, by contrast, *is* expressible: two argmaxes over a shared feasible set, the temptation value subtracted.)
3. **Smooth ambiguity, non-exponential φ (Klibanoff–Marinacci–Mukerji 2005).** A nested `φ ∘ 𝔼 ∘ φ⁻¹` over a prior axis; the exponential-φ case is `LogitChoiceStage` at `ε<0`, a general φ is not. A sibling of Epstein–Zin (both nonlinear certainty equivalents). *Status:* ✗ missing primitive.

#### B — generalized choice apply
4. **General convex choice cost beyond entropy (G3 — sparsemax / Tsallis / general Bregman).** `LogitChoiceStage` implements exactly the *entropy*-regularized soft choice (`ε<0` the soft-min); `ArgmaxStage` the hard limit. A perturbed-utility choice with a *different* convex cost — consideration sets, zero-probability actions (Caplin–Dean 2015), sparsemax / q-logit posteriors — is a distinct apply not yet built. *Status:* ✗ missing primitive (a general-convex-cost soft-choice generalizing `LogitChoiceStage`).
5. **Coupled multi-signal attention budget (Maćkowiak–Wiederholt 2009).** One Shannon attention budget *across several signals* couples multiple precisions through a single constraint; each single-signal precision choice is `ScaleVarianceStage` (✅), but the joint budget is not one univariate stage. *Status:* ◐/✗ (a coupled-constraint generalization).
6. **Bargaining / terms-of-trade stage (Lagos–Wright 2005, decentralized market).** Generalized Nash bargaining sets the terms of trade by maximizing a **product** objective `[u(q)−φd]^θ·[φd−c(q)]^{1−θ}` — a fundamentally different apply from the entropy/Bregman soft-choice of items 4–5, and from the additive `reward + βV_end` of every existing choice stage. It further mixes three exogenous role-conditional (buyer / seller / no-match) money-transition maps that no combinator expresses. The dogfooding §0 pass surfaced this as the one genuine missing *within-period* primitive in the monetary spine (the CM leg is `ConsumptionSavingsStage`; money-market clearing is outer-loop). *Status:* ✗ missing primitive (a product-form Nash-bargaining / terms-of-trade choice apply with role-conditional transition mixing).

#### C — subjective-backward ≠ objective-forward kernel
7. **Active-learning / subjective ≠ objective kernel (Kennan–Walker 2011; Arcidiacono 2004).** A Bayesian active-learning belief update is a *stochastic* kernel whose signal distribution is **choice-dependent**, and under anticipated-utility the agent's **subjective** backward kernel (how it *values* the continuation) differs from the **objective** forward kernel (how the measure actually evolves). A single `HouseholdStages` stage carries **one** kernel used as both `Kᵀ` (backward on `V`) and `K` (forward on `Λ`), so it cannot represent a stage whose backward and forward kernels differ. This is its own kind of gap — neither a non-affine value-aggregator (family A) nor a generalized choice apply (family B), but a stage with **distinct subjective-backward / objective-forward kernels**. The destination-choice sub-block is fine (a clean `MigrationStage`/`LogitChoiceStage`); only the learning is blocked. *Status:* ✗ missing primitive (a stage with distinct subjective-backward / objective-forward kernels).

Everything else in the catalog is either a clean household/agent block (✅/◐) or an outer-loop object out of scope for all models.

### Takeaway

Essentially every canonical heterogeneous-agent **household block** in the literature is a `∘`/`⊕`
composition of existing `HouseholdStages` stages — across all six control mechanisms. The framework's
consistent stance: *what the household controls about its transition* (its drift, dispersion, hazard,
direction, or a discrete jump) is a **stage**; *the market or planner object that those choices feed*
(a price that clears, a tightness from free entry, a two-sided assignment, an attention prior, an
optimal contract, the cross-sectional distribution itself) is the **outer loop** — which this package
provides for *no* model (it builds household blocks), so it is never scored against a block. The honest
◐s are where an auxiliary belief/choice axis or a cross-period/cross-generation composition is needed
(no missing primitive). The only genuine **household-stage** gaps are three small families: a non-affine
value/time aggregator (Epstein–Zin, present-bias), a generalized choice apply (G3), and a stage with
distinct subjective-backward / objective-forward kernels (Bayesian active learning). And the reach is wider than
households: the *same* stages express **firm dynamics** under one dictionary (§6 — `capital↔wealth`,
`scrap↔bequest`, `exit↔death`, `menu cost↔(S,s) durable`, `inattentive pricing↔RI`), and every signed
